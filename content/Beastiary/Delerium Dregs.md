“One minute you’re getting a foot rub from your royal masseuse Peter and suddenly, because you asked him to grab something sharp to carve off the dead skin, he’s transformed into a blobby monster. These poor souls used to be so important to my daily routine. My sword fetcher, my helmet fetcher, my servant fetcher, heck, even my flower arranger all turned into wretched twisted creatures. Good help is hard to find but when they become an amorphous killing machine bent on consuming your flesh it’s time to find some new hires.”

—Pluto Jackson

When a human is exposed to enough eldritch contamination to cause a monstrous transformation, the ensuing cascade of mutations most commonly results in a horrific mockery of their former selves. Countless inhabitants of Drakkenheim have suffered this horrible fate, transformed after the meteor fell. Others are fallen soldiers, lost adventurers, or faction agents who never escaped the ruins. Survivors and explorers often refer to these creatures as “wretches,” “unfortunates,” “the lost ones,” but they are most commonly known as Delerium Dregs.

These monsters are hideously transfigured in both body and mind. They are trapped in a terrifying landscape filled with delusions and torment, left with only fragmented memories, unable to comprehend what has happened to them. Delerium Dregs have been driven mad by their transformations.

Occasionally, a Delerium Dreg may still be recognizable to their friends and loved ones, but are so stricken with madness that little remains of their former personalities. Others have changed so much they bear no resemblance to their former selves, though most retain some humanoid features and body proportions. No matter how they ended up in this horrific form, they are doomed to wander the ruins, disorientation occasionally wavering into anguish, distress, or violent outbursts.

## [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#MultitudinousMutations)Multitudinous Mutations

No two Delerium Dregs are exactly alike, as all are transformed in chaotic and unpredictable ways. Their bodies sprout all manner of twisted limbs, spines, and bones. Their organs, muscles, and flesh are resculpted, their faces twisted painfully into terrifying visages. Nevertheless, there are several recognizable and relatively common variations which all express a differing degree of danger to those exploring Drakkenheim.

Those who have studied eldritch contamination and mutations have theorized that certain factors may influence the types of mutations a contaminated individual may manifest. Perhaps the subject’s physical or biological traits are the determining factor, especially in those who may already possess magical talents or who have been touched by arcane forces in some way. It may also be that the environment and conditions under which an individual gains **[Contamination](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/contamination-delerium-and-new-conditions#ContaminationCondition)** affect the forms they develop, as [Bloated Dregs](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5524952-bloated-dreg) appear more commonly near flooded regions, and [Lambent Dregs](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525049-lambent-dreg) are more common near the crater. It is also entirely possible it is all simply chaotic magic, and any patterns are mere coincidence.

Indeed, a [Delerium Dreg](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5524979-delerium-dreg) continues to mutate with continued exposure to eldritch contamination. Over time, the typical Delerium Dreg described here may change into one of the other forms. However, these continued mutations may eventually cause their form to destabilize, transforming them into a [Protean Abomination](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525081-protean-abomination) or [Pyknic Maunder](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525083-pyknic-maunder).

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#WarpedMinds)Warped Minds

While their new form may hold some uncanny resemblance to their original appearance, most often, a Delerium Dreg’s mutations are so drastic and their behavior so radically altered that little trace is left of their former identity. Most Dregs wander Drakkenheim unfettered from reality and are easily provoked to violence.

Experienced soldiers amongst the Hooded Lanterns have observed how Dregs passively follow one another. As they aimlessly roam the ruined streets, more stragglers and lone dregs join a group, which eventually gathers into a large crowd. While occasionally infighting or hostility breaks out within the throng, left unchecked in the city, Delerium Dregs slowly start to form massive mobs that clog the city streets.

[Hooded Lantern scouts](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525036-hooded-lantern-scout) have adamantly patrolled the western parts of the city near their barracks, slaying any small group of Dregs on sight. If they can keep their numbers down, it is less likely that uncontrollable swarms of them will gather. Dreg cleanup is one of the most harrowing duties for the Lanterns. More often than not, the Dregs in western Drakkenheim are fallen Lanterns, friends, or even family.

Adventurers incorrectly believe Delerium Dregs are cannibalistic, driven to feed on flesh. All evidence suggests Delerium Dregs are physically sustained by the magic of the Haze. They do not need to eat or drink, and do not sleep. They do, however, need to breathe. Consequently, they do not consume the bodies of their victims; rather, they attack out of madness-induced aggression. As such, a Delerium Dreg won’t be lured into a trap by meat and usually can’t be bargained with using food.

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#HazeHulks)Haze Hulks

Occasionaly, these transformations include tremendous growth. Although studies of this are scarce, it seems that a common change is an instant increase in both muscle mass and ferocity. These violent monstrosities are known as [Haze Hulks](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525021-haze-hulk), huge cousins to the twisted Delerium Dregs.

The delerium affects the flesh of the creature, immediately toughening a creature and increasing its inherent strength, while diminishing parts of the brain that process emotions such as anger or frustration, and completely neutralizing the concepts of fear and greatly diminishing pain. The effects of this are a massive hulking beast that shrugs off attacks, is violently territorial, and shows no fear in the face of danger.

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#AdvancedMutations)Advanced Mutations

Some creatures grow beyond the confines of standard mutations, where a rare few no longer fit into the obvious categories of Delerium Dregs or Haze Hulks. These creatures possess traits that separate them from the mutated hordes of the ruins, often because of their ability to maintain control of their sentience and sense of self in a way most others cannot.

The danger occurs when these creatures believe they are still human despite terrible transformations. These advanced mutants are often twisted by misguided goals, such as gaining control over their mutations. Others are just unfortunates who cling to the shreds of self remaining in their slowly fragmenting minds. These may be the most dangerous of all. They still possess a cunning intellect while slipping into a madness they cannot fathom.

Although rare, encountering these forms of advanced mutations is always dangerous—they have been known to control Dregs, or unite the mutated monsters to fight on their behalf. Two of these advanced mutants are described in this chapter: the malfeasant wizard known as the [Pale Man](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525076-pale-man), and the former Queen of Drakkenheim, [Lenore von Kessel](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5525051-lenore-von-kessel).

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#ShatteredPsyches)Shattered Psyches

Rookie adventurers often liken [Delerium Dregs](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5524979-delerium-dreg) to the mindless undead such as zombies. While the comparison is apt in some respects, Delerium Dregs are not brainless creatures or reanimated thralls. Rather, these mutated humans retain a broken consciousness drifting in and out of a lucid state. These horrible shreds of sentience can be difficult to witness, as the once human creatures mumble phrases of their last moments. Some cry for help, others beg for safety, while others call to loved ones and old companions. There are a few reports of Delerium Dregs have apparently coherent conversations for a few minutes before returning to their incoherent mumbles and ravenous cries.

Over the years, many have attempted to communicate with Delerium Dregs in search of their former humanity. Occasionally, Delerium Dregs respond to such appeals, but the effect is short-lived. The madness gripping their minds is all-consuming and isn’t readily treated with words or even powerful magic. More than once have well-intentioned individuals sought out a transformed friend or loved one and tried to coax them back into a cogent state, grasping at whatever shreds of humanity remain. Unfortunately, these attempts ultimately fail; at best gleaning a fleeting moment of recognition before the terrifying realization of their wretched state becomes apparent. Often, such efforts only succeed in triggering frenzied outbursts, but there is no doubt that a handful of Dregs are kept captive in the hopes that one day their condition may somehow be healed or reversed.

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#LastingMemories)Lasting Memories

Dregs may not be coherent, or even human anymore, but a small sliver of their former selves remains. Many have strange quirks that reveal their former selves, often displayed while tearing their victims apart.

1. In a deep, bellowing tone, the Dreg exclaims “Meat.”
2. It begs for release, “Help me! I need to get out of this city!”
3. “Please, please, help! I’m sick... are you a doctor? Bring me to the Flamekeeper!”
4. It recites a list of its former comrades-in-arms: “Jenkins. Watson. Lang. Wagner. Livingston. Hayes.”
5. “Miserable weather. Just dreadful. Awful. Won’t stop raining. When will we see the sun?”
6. It cries out for its parents or family “Where is my daddy? I want my daddy!”
7. It hums or whistles fragments of songs from time to time.
8. It won’t stop giggling horrifically. Between laughs, it says “Who wants tickles?!?”
9. It keeps speaking about a plan it had, such as an event, or party it was meant to attend. “I’m going to be late for the masquerade ball! I have my finest suit pressed and ready.”
10. It seems confused and lost and keeps requesting directions to a specific location. “Do I turn left at Copper Street? Where’s the wainwright’s shop? Around the corner?”
11. It screams accusations at the top of its lungs constantly “Thief! You dirty thief!”
12. The Dreg recites a children’s nursery rhyme or poem with an eerie, echoing cadence: “Ashes, ashes, we all fall down...”
13. “I’m late... I’m late... oh bother... I should have left sooner... the Queen will have my head for sure.”
14. The Dreg gestures or points at itself, crying out “Guilty! Guilty! Guilty!”
15. It regards you as a long-lost family member. “Are you my mommy?”
16. “Where did I leave my glasses? I thought they were right here... there was time now...”
17. It apologizes constantly and profusely. “I’m sorry, I am so sorry!”
18. This Dreg used to be an old construction worker who keeps mumbling “Tear down the wall, tear down the wall...”
19. The Dreg chases you yelling in a cackling screech “I’ll swallow your soul!”
20. “We had porridge yesterday! I don’t want porridge again. We had it yesterday!”
21. The Dreg holds a large wooden spoon and keeps repeating in a defeated voice “My spoon is too big.”
22. As you cut into the Dreg with your weapon, it says in a satisfied voice, “I like it when the red water comes out.”
23. The Dreg follows you slowly, in a monotone voice repeating “Come play with me.”
24. The Dreg seems fearful of its surroundings and keeps looking at the rooftops with darting eyes muttering “Can’t stop here, this is rat country.”

## [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#LocationsandLairs)Locations and Lairs

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#TheStreetsofDrakkenheim)The Streets of Drakkenheim

Dregs do not build lairs or burrows like many of the other creatures that occupy Drakkenheim. They do not have a specific preference to the types of environments they inhabit. Once a creature succumbs to **[Contamination](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/contamination-delerium-and-new-conditions#ContaminationCondition)** and is lost, they stay within the Haze and tend to gravitate to areas of contaminated magic, such as delerium clusters and Thin Places. However, they can be found almost anywhere throughout the city. These Dregs wander in groups throughout Drakkenheim, attacking anything not touched by Contamination.

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#DarkCornersandOldRuins)Dark Corners and Old Ruins

Many Dregs tend to stick to shadowy areas, such as old ruins or abandoned buildings. They wander through the dimly lit streets often enough, but any venture into an old building is likely to find a conglomerate of various Dregs.

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#GatheringattheSource)Gathering at the Source

Dregs will gather around clusters of delerium. It is unclear why they are instinctually pulled to delerium. Perhaps because Dregs were initially created by the **[Contamination](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/contamination-delerium-and-new-conditions#ContaminationCondition)** produced by delerium, they constantly seek more as a means of fulfilling some sort of advanced transformation. Regardless of why, large groups of Dregs have been known to gather around clusters and remain in the area.

## [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#CombatTactics)Combat Tactics

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#PredatoryInstincts)Predatory Instincts

Few Delerium Dregs possess the clarity of mind to execute any strategy or tactic beyond pure direct aggression. However, a few are patient predators who are more cunning in their approaches. Some of the smarter, or larger, Dregs will stalk their prey and know to stick to the shadows, hiding behind rubble. In very rare instances, Dregs may plan an ambush.

### [](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/modr/delerium-dregs#HerdingCreatures)Herding Creatures

It is rare for a Dreg to be found alone, unless it has recently transformed. Dregs who find one another tend to band together and form wandering groups. Often the largest or smartest of the Dregs will lead the group, while others follow aimlessly. In the instances where Dregs surprise and ambush an adventuring group, it is often a smaller group of smarter Dregs.

The rank and file soldiers in the now twisted city huddle together to attack those who haven’t changed yet. Don’t become one, use your sword.
