## Tournament Framework

Each tournament has a theme, a random enemy table, and up to five rounds. The party can stop after any round, but later rounds offer better rewards and greater danger.

### Basic Structure

Before the tournament begins, determine:

- **Theme:** undead, beasts, constructs, fungi, cultists, elementals, and so on.
- **Arena:** tavern basement, quarry pit, ruined temple, sewer, forest clearing, or magical demiplane.
- **Sponsor:** guild, noble, criminal faction, Duster, wizard, or extraplanar being.
- **Reward:** gold, magic items, faction favor, information, or the right to select the next opponent.
- **Maximum Rounds:** usually 3 to 5.

Each round should include:

1. A random enemy group.
2. A distinct arena complication.
3. A reward for surviving.
4. A choice to continue or withdraw.

## Increasing Difficulty

Use the party’s level and number of characters to determine the approximate difficulty of each round.

| Round | Difficulty | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy or Medium | Establish the theme and arena |
| 2 | Medium | Introduce a tactical complication |
| 3 | Hard | Add stronger enemies or a dangerous hazard |
| 4 | Very Hard | Combine enemy abilities and arena effects |
| 5 | Deadly or Boss | Final champion, monster pair, or special objective |

For a level 4 party, a simple progression could be:

- **Round 1:** 400 to 600 adjusted XP
- **Round 2:** 600 to 900 adjusted XP
- **Round 3:** 900 to 1,200 adjusted XP
- **Round 4:** 1,200 to 1,600 adjusted XP
- **Round 5:** 1,600 to 2,200 adjusted XP

These numbers are only a starting point. The arena hazard can make a fight substantially harder without adding more creatures.

## Random Enemy Tables

Each theme should have several enemy categories rather than one list of unrelated monsters.

### Example: Undead Tournament

| d6 | Enemy Group |
|---:|---|
| 1 | 4 Skeletons |
| 2 | 2 Zombies and 2 Skeletons |
| 3 | 3 Ghouls |
| 4 | 1 Wight or elite undead champion |
| 5 | 2 Specters and 2 Skeletons |
| 6 | A random undead group chosen by the DM |

The round determines how many times you roll:

- **Round 1:** Roll once
- **Round 2:** Roll once, then add one minor minion
- **Round 3:** Roll twice and combine the groups
- **Round 4:** Roll twice, then add an arena hazard
- **Round 5:** Roll once for the boss and once for its support creatures

This lets you reuse the same table while creating stronger combinations later.

## Themed Tournament Ideas

### The Graveyard Shift

Undead opponents fight in a cemetery arena filled with open graves, mausoleums, and cursed lanterns.

Possible enemies:

- Skeletons
- Zombies
- Ghouls
- Specters
- Wights
- Mummies
- A possessed corpse of a former champion

Arena effects:

- Open graves create difficult terrain.
- Unattended corpses rise after 2 rounds.
- Radiant damage temporarily suppresses undead reinforcements.
- The audience is composed of ghosts who cheer whenever someone dies.

### The Sporeshroom Slam

The party fights fungal creatures in an arena gradually overtaken by aggressive mushrooms.

Possible enemies:

- Violet fungi
- Gas spores
- Myconids
- Shambling mounds
- Rot-themed beasts
- Cultists wearing fungal growths

Arena effects:

- Spore clouds create obscured areas.
- Mushrooms grow from blood spilled on the floor.
- Characters who fail Constitution saves begin hearing commentary from the fungus.
- A giant mushroom announces the score but keeps getting the names wrong.

### The Scrapheap Rumble

Constructs and magitech creatures fight in a junkyard arena.

Possible enemies:

- Animated armor
- Flying swords
- Rug of smothering
- Helmed horrors
- Clockwork creatures
- A patched-together machine champion

Arena effects:

- Moving platforms and conveyor belts.
- Random magical discharges.
- Cover from piles of scrap.
- A destroyed construct may explode or release a useful component.

### The Beast Pit

The party fights increasingly dangerous animals and monstrosities in a shifting wilderness arena.

Possible enemies:

- Wolves
- Giant spiders
- Dire wolves
- Owlbears
- Displacer beasts
- Bulette
- Griffon or wyvern champion

Arena effects:

- The terrain changes between forest, swamp, cliffside, and open field.
- The beasts may attack each other if provoked.
- Some creatures can be subdued instead of killed.
- A handler occasionally throws food into the arena, causing a creature to change targets.

### The Cult of the Worst Idea

The party fights cultists who have misunderstood their own religion.

Possible enemies:

- Cultists
- A cult fanatic
- A possessed commoner
- Animated religious objects
- Summoned demons or undead
- A failed cult leader transformed by their own ritual

Arena effects:

- Cultists activate one ritual effect each round.
- The ritual can be disrupted by destroying candles, runes, or sacrificial objects.
- Some enemies surrender if their leader is defeated.
- The announcer insists every round is part of a sacred prophecy.

### Arcannabis Cup

The party fights enemies affected by different Arcannabis strains. Each round introduces a new effect.

Possible enemies:

- Bandits
- Animated plants
- Fungal creatures
- Beasts under strain effects
- Undead infused with magical plant matter
- A champion combining several strains

Arena effects:

- Colored smoke creates different temporary effects.
- One strain grants speed.
- One grants resistance.
- One causes hallucinations.
- One makes creatures overly confident and reckless.
- The audience votes on which strain is released next.

## Round Rewards

Give the players a reason to continue, but make withdrawal meaningful.

| Round Completed | Suggested Reward |
|---|---|
| 1 | 25 gp each, food, lodging, or minor consumable |
| 2 | 50 gp each, faction favor, or common magic item |
| 3 | 100 gp each, uncommon consumable, or useful information |
| 4 | 150 gp each, uncommon magic item, or major favor |
| 5 | 300 gp each, rare story award, or signature prize |

The rewards should be paid per character when appropriate. Large prizes, such as a magic item or faction favor, should be recorded as party or story rewards.

## Player Choices Between Rounds

After each round, let the players choose one benefit:

- **Recover:** Each character can spend Hit Dice.
- **Prepare:** Gain advantage on one skill check during the next round.
- **Study:** Learn one resistance, vulnerability, immunity, or special ability of the next enemy.
- **Sabotage:** Alter one arena feature before the next fight.
- **Bet:** Risk some of the current reward for a chance to double it.
- **Withdraw:** Keep the rewards already earned and avoid the next round.

This makes the tournament more than five consecutive combats.

## Random Complications

At the beginning of each round, roll a d8:

| d8 | Complication |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The arena becomes difficult terrain |
| 2 | One section becomes hazardous |
| 3 | The enemies enter from multiple directions |
| 4 | The audience demands a special objective |
| 5 | A random combatant gains temporary hit points |
| 6 | One enemy has a useful environmental advantage |
| 7 | A defeated creature returns with half its hit points |
| 8 | The arena changes halfway through the fight |

## Special Tournament Objectives

Not every round needs to be won by killing everything.

- Protect a fragile object for 3 rounds.
- Capture one enemy alive.
- Prevent enemies from reaching the audience.
- Destroy three ritual anchors.
- Rescue a hostage.
- Collect a monster component before the round ends.
- Defeat the champion without using lethal damage.
- Survive for 5 rounds while reinforcements arrive.
- Identify which enemy is secretly an illusion.
- Win while keeping the arena mostly undamaged.
