*A Tier 1 Free League of Stickyickistan adventure for four 2nd-level characters*

# Introduction

**The Shining Shrine** is a fey-themed monster hunt set in [[Springwood]], a sacred woodland and naturalized park in [[Stickyickistan]]. The adventure is optimized for four 2nd-level characters and is designed to take three to four hours.

The characters are hired to protect [[Springarden]], a holy garden maintained by the [[Blossom Union]]. A fey owlbear has claimed the garden’s shrine, corrupted its calming waters, driven away pilgrims, and begun feeding on the shrine’s sacred koi. The hunt culminates in a three-wave battle against the owlbear and the fey creatures it has drawn into its lair.

## Adventure Background

The barrier between the Material Plane and the Plane of Fey is unusually thin around Springarden. Small fey creatures pass freely through the boundary, and the garden remains in perpetual spring. Each year, a celestial alignment weakens the barrier further during the festival known as Feyfest.

This year, the alignment drew a **suneater owlbear** from the Plane of Fey. The owlbear is accustomed to feeding on the weak sunlight of the Feywild’s eternal sunset. The Material Plane’s midday sun is far more nourishing, but the owlbear did not account for the long hours of darkness that follow it. During the night, its hunger becomes unbearable.

The suneater owlbear made its lair at Springarden’s holiest shrine. During the day, it feeds on sunlight. At night, it hunts the garden’s giant flying koi and lashes out at anyone who approaches. Its hunger and territorial rage have twisted Springarden’s soothing magic, causing visitors and animals to become unusually aggressive.

The [[Blossom Union]] depends on pilgrims, visitors, and festival offerings to maintain Springarden. If the owlbear remains, the garden will lose its pilgrims and the surrounding woodland may be contaminated by its rage.

## Adventure Hook

The characters find the following public notice in [[The Rolling Papers]].

> **SPRINGARDEN SEEKS LICENSED MONSTER HUNTERS**
>
> Pilgrims and travelers are advised to avoid the holy gardens of [[Springwood]] until further notice. A dangerous fey creature has occupied the Shining Shrine, injured members of the [[Blossom Union]], disturbed the sacred waters, and driven away visitors.
>
> The Blossom Union offers payment to capable adventurers who can identify the creature, protect Springarden, and remove the threat without damaging the shrine or its sacred pools.
>
> Applicants should report to [[The Loot Tavern]] and ask for **[[Threeflower]]**. He will provide the necessary travel instructions and explain how to reach Springarden.

## Part 1: The Port Ale

*Estimated Duration: 30 minutes*

The characters must first obtain a bottle of Port Ale, the unusual drink required to enter [[The Loot Tavern]]. They can purchase it from a licensed tavern, an Arcannabis merchant, or another source in Stickyickistan. Each member of the party needs their own bottle.

### Finding Port Ale

The characters can locate a seller with a successful DC 11 Charisma (Persuasion), Intelligence (Investigation), or Wisdom (Survival) check. If they ask around in a busy drinking district, a seller approaches them before they finish searching and offers a bottle for 5 gp.

If the characters lack the money, they can earn or bargain for the bottle by completing a small favor for the seller, such as delivering a message, retrieving a misplaced crate, or dealing with an aggressive customer. Do not turn this into a full adventure; the purpose of the scene is to establish the drink and give the characters a clear route into the Loot Tavern.

### Entering the Loot Tavern

Once the characters have the [[Port Ale]], they enter any ordinary tavern and drink it. Within one minute, they must step through that tavern’s door. The door becomes a passage to the exterior of the drifting [[Astral Plane]] tavern, delivering them to [[The Loot Tavern]], where **Threeflower** is waiting for them.

Read or paraphrase:

> The bottle’s last drop vanishes from the glass, and the world lurches sideways.
>
> For one dizzying moment, the street becomes a tunnel of golden light. Then you are standing in a crowded tavern filled with adventurers, impossible trophies, and the smell of food from places you have never visited.
>
> A nervous gnome in roughspun clothes raises a hand from a nearby table. A many-eyed squirrel peeks out from his pocket.
>
> “Oh! You made it. Good. I was worried the Port Ale instructions might have been unclear.”

## Threeflower

Threeflower is a timid gnome druid and a member of the Blossom Union. He acts as the party’s guide and primary contact.

**Personality.** Nervous in new company, relaxed in nature, kind, and grateful.

**Appearance.** Stocky, with moss-green hair and roughspun clothing decorated with naturally moulted feathers and fur. He is accompanied by a blink squirrel named Moo.

**Desire.** To find a quiet, sunlit corner and spend his evenings smoking half-leaf.

**Fear.** Offending people, confrontation, and forest fires.

> “Yawns* Oh, h-h-hello. Am I in your way? No? Sorry. Moo would like to know if you have any candy.”

Threeflower explains that the owlbear has occupied the shrine, injured several druids, and caused aggressive behavior throughout the garden. He offers the characters payment collected from pilgrims and asks them to return the shrine to a safe condition.

After the briefing, Threeflower guides the characters from the Loot Tavern to [[Springarden]].

## Content Warning

Fey horror, monster hunting, radiant damage, blinding effects, aggressive mood changes, body horror, and a prolonged multi-stage combat encounter.

# Part 2: Journey to Springarden

*Estimated Duration: 45 minutes*

Threeflower guides the characters from [[The Loot Tavern]] into [[Springwood]]. The journey uses three Tracking checks, and the party must achieve at least one success to reach Springarden. On a successful check, describe a strange fey encounter or an unusual feature of the forest. On a failed check, run a hostile encounter from the Springwood or forest encounter tables. The characters still progress after a failure, but they arrive at Springarden with fewer resources or alert fey creatures nearby.

## Tracking the Road to Springarden

Ask the players how they travel and what they look for. They may follow the road, study changing vegetation, question fey creatures, watch for signs of the owlbear, or rely on Threeflower’s knowledge of the forest. Choose an ability and skill or tool that fits their approach, then set a DC based on the method.

Use the following guidance:

- **DC 11:** Following the well-traveled forest road, navigating by Threeflower’s directions, or identifying the signs of perpetual spring.
- **DC 13:** Finding safe paths around fey crossings, recognizing dangerous plants, or locating fresh owlbear tracks.
- **DC 16:** Crossing an overgrown route quickly, interpreting a fey creature’s warning, or tracking the owlbear’s movements from damaged trees and scattered feathers.

A character may use Wisdom (Survival), Wisdom (Perception), Intelligence (Nature), Intelligence (Arcana), Charisma (Animal Handling), or another suitable check supported by the party’s plan. Do not require every character to make the same check. Encourage different approaches.

## Tracking Consequences

On a successful check, the characters notice a useful feature of Springwood or encounter a harmless fey creature. Give them one of the clues in the Investigation Scenes section if appropriate.

On a failed check, the party has a hostile encounter or suffers a complication. Choose or roll one:

| d4 | Complication |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A fey creature misdirects the party, adding several hours to the journey. |
| 2 | The party crosses a faerie ring and must find its way back to the road. |
| 3 | Aggressive forest creatures attack after being disturbed by the owlbear’s magic. |
| 4 | A sudden Feywild storm fills the road with illusions and separates the party briefly. |

When the party completes three Tracking checks and has at least one success, Threeflower leads them to Springarden.

## Arrival at Springarden


Read or paraphrase:

> The forest changes as you approach Springarden. Dry leaves become green shoots, green shoots become summer foliage, and then every branch erupts into blossom. Warm mist rises from clear pools scattered among mossy islands and flowering trees.
>
> Tiny winged creatures flit between flowers, leaving trails of sparkling dust. The air smells of rain, pollen, and warm stone.
>
> Yet the garden is almost empty. Bridges stand undisturbed, offerings lie abandoned beside the paths, and several pools are clouded by feathers, scales, and half-eaten fish.
>
> A nervous gnome waits at the gate beside a blink squirrel with far too many eyes.

## Springarden

Springarden is a sprawling sacred garden in the heart of [[Springwood]]. It contains hot springs, clear pools, flowering islands, bridges, stepping stones, shrines, and small sanctuaries used by the Blossom Union.

The garden’s natural magic normally calms visitors and encourages healing. The suneater owlbear’s presence has twisted that magic. Visitors may experience sudden anger, suspicion, or reckless courage, especially near the shrine.

### General Features

- **Ceilings, Walls, and Floors.** The garden is open to the sky. There are no walls, and the ground is a mixture of soil, stone paths, bridges, and small islands.
- **Light.** The light depends on the time of day but always has a pleasant golden quality reminiscent of sunset. At night, the garden is dark unless illuminated by fey creatures or the shrine.
- **Atmosphere.** The air is warm, the breeze is gentle, and the flowers release a calming perfume.
- **Sacred Pools.** The pools contain giant flying koi and are central to the garden’s religious significance.

## Investigation Scenes

Use two or three scenes before the characters approach the shrine. Each scene provides a clue about the owlbear or the final encounter.

### Pyrax’s Feather

Pyrax, a lizardfolk druid of the Blossom Union, is unconscious in the healer’s hut after confronting the owlbear. Pyrax has milky eyes, severe radiant burns, and a purple feather clutched in one hand.

A successful DC 13 Wisdom (Medicine) check reveals that Pyrax has been blinded and suffered severe radiant damage. The hand holding the feather is unburned, proving that the feather was removed before the radiant attack.

The feather is not damaged by radiant damage. Damage it takes repairs itself when exposed to bright sunlight.

**Clue:** The owlbear deals radiant damage, is immune to radiant damage, and can recover in bright sunlight.

### Feyfest Flyer

Feyfest flyers are posted throughout Springarden. They show a five-star constellation arranged as a cross:

- A gold star in the center.
- A black star to the north.
- A red star to the south.
- A white star to the west.
- A blue star to the east.

Threeflower explains that the constellation weakens the boundary between the Material Plane and the Feywild.

**Clue:** The constellation’s arrangement is needed to solve the radiant cocoon puzzle during the second wave.

### Fey Creatures

The closer the characters get to Springarden’s shrine, the fewer ordinary beasts they see and the more fey creatures appear. A blink squirrel, sprite, or other Tiny fey creature may refuse to respond to spells that affect beasts.

Threeflower explains that small fey creatures cross into Springarden regularly. The suneater owlbear is also fey, not a beast, and cannot be affected by magic that specifically targets beasts.

**Clue:** The owlbear is a fey creature and should be treated accordingly.

## Know Thy Enemy

Threeflower and the Blossom Union can provide the following information:

- The owlbear deals radiant damage and can blind creatures.
- It is immune to radiant damage and resistant to fire damage.
- Bright sunlight restores the owlbear’s strength.
- It becomes most dangerous when hungry at night.
- The owlbear is a fey creature, not a beast.
- Several smaller fey creatures now follow it around the shrine.
- The shrine’s magic may protect the owlbear after it is defeated once.

# Part 2: The Shining Shrine

*Estimated Duration: 90–120 minutes*

The characters approach the owlbear’s lair and begin the three-wave encounter.

## Approaching the Shrine

Show the players the map of the Shining Shrine and read or paraphrase the appropriate description.

### During the Day

> In front of the moss-covered shrine lies a mound of purple feathers, basking beneath the midday sun. Its mouth hangs open, and its stillness might be peaceful if it were not surrounded by half-eaten koi and fish heads.

### At Night

> In front of the moss-covered shrine lies a mound of purple feathers, apparently asleep. Its gentle snoring is surrounded by the wet sounds of feeding, and half-eaten koi are scattered across the shrine’s steps.

## Emotive Waters

A creature other than the owlbear that enters one of Springarden’s pools or starts its turn there must make a DC 13 Charisma saving throw.

On a failure, roll a d6:

- **Even result:** The creature is affected by *calm emotions* until the end of its next turn and becomes indifferent toward other creatures.
- **Odd result:** The creature becomes enraged until the end of its next turn and must use its reaction to make one attack against a randomly determined creature within range, if possible.

## Lair Actions

While the owlbear is alive and within the shrine, it can use the garden’s magic to create one of the following effects on initiative count 20. The same effect cannot be used two rounds in a row.

- **Rage.** Each other creature in the lair must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or use its reaction to make one weapon attack against a randomly determined creature within reach or range.
- **Disorienting Lights.** Each creature other than the owlbear in a 20-foot cube must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or be affected by confusion until the end of its next turn.
- **Summon Fey Lights.** A hostile fey light appears beside each creature of the owlbear’s choice. The lights disappear at the next initiative count 20.

## Wave 1: Sunbear

The first wave begins when the characters enter the shrine’s grounds.

### Enemies

Use the appropriate suneater owlbear stat block from the adventure’s appendix or source material. If using the 5.5e Monster Manual only, use an **Owlbear** as the base creature and describe its attacks and abilities as radiant and fey-themed.

Add fey support creatures according to the party’s level:

- **2nd level:** 1 Owlbear.
- **7th level:** 1 Owlbear and 2 Sprites.
- **12th level:** 1 Owlbear and 2 Pixies.

The owlbear is territorial and does not retreat. It uses the shrine, pools, bridges, and water to divide the party.

## Wave 2: Constellation

When the owlbear is reduced to 0 hit points, a golden cocoon forms around its body and four colored orbs appear around it.

Read or paraphrase:

> A golden forcefield blooms around the fallen owlbear. Its body becomes a faint silhouette within the sphere as four glowing orbs coalesce in the air: white, black, red, and blue.
>
> The orbs drift around the cocoon, each pulsing with a different light. The shrine’s symbols begin to glow in response.

The characters can push the orbs. They must arrange them around the cocoon according to the Feyfest constellation:

- Black to the north.
- Blue to the east.
- Red to the south.
- White to the west.

When the orbs are correctly arranged, the cocoon disappears and the owlbear enters its second form. Characters who remember the flyer or discovered the constellation clue know the correct solution.

The cocoon lasts for 1 hour. While inside it, the owlbear is incapacitated, has a speed of 0 feet, and cannot be affected by creatures or effects outside the cocoon. If the characters take too long, the owlbear recovers and emerges without gaining its mythic benefits.

If the characters cannot solve the puzzle, the cocoon can be attacked. It has AC 10, hit points equal to 50 times the party’s average level, and immunity to fire, poison, psychic, and radiant damage. Whenever the cocoon takes damage, the attacker or spellcaster takes radiant damage.

## Wave 3: Supernova

When the cocoon is deactivated, the owlbear rises in its mythic form. Use the same owlbear statistics as in Wave 1, adding the source material’s mythic abilities if available.

At the beginning of this wave, read or paraphrase:

> The cocoon tears open with a sound like breaking glass. A miniature sun ignites above the owlbear’s head, and its feathers erupt in a blinding crown of radiant light.
>
> The creature rises, no longer hungry or afraid. It is furious.

At higher levels, add official fey or plant creatures:

- **7th level:** 1 Shambling Mound.
- **12th level:** 2 Shambling Mounds.

The owlbear seeks to blind and burn as many characters as possible. It no longer retreats or protects itself.

## Objectives Beyond Defeat

The characters may also:

- Protect the shrine from unnecessary damage.
- Keep the owlbear away from the sacred pools.
- Rescue injured Blossom Union members.
- Prevent the flying koi from being killed.
- Recover the shrine’s offerings without stealing them.

# Part 3: Aftermath

*Estimated Duration: 30 minutes*

When the owlbear is defeated, the garden’s rage-inducing magic recedes over the following 24 hours. The pools clear, the koi return to their usual behavior, and the fey creatures begin crossing the boundary peacefully again.

The Blossom Union holds a feast in the characters’ honor and names them heroes of Springarden. The Union offers each character the agreed payment and grants the party access to Springarden’s hot springs.

The party may receive one magic item from the shrine’s offerings, chosen by the Blossom Union. Taking additional items without permission is theft. If the characters steal from the shrine, Archdruid Xyxl uses *locate object* to recover the items and bans the characters from Springarden unless they work as gardeners for one month as penance.

## Rewards

- Payment from the Blossom Union.
- One shrine magic item chosen by the Union.
- Access to Springarden’s hot springs.
- Favor with the Blossom Union.
- Fey monster components, if the party safely harvests them.
- A potential teleportation-circle connection to Springarden.

## Further Adventures

- A young suneater owlbear arrives in Springarden and must be returned to its mother in the Feywild.
- A section of Springarden has turned to autumn and attacks anyone who enters it.
- The constellation appears in the sky out of season, opening a dangerous new passage to the Plane of Fey.
- A faction attempts to steal the shrine’s offerings while the Blossom Union celebrates.

# Difficulty and Scaling

For four 2nd-level characters, use only the Owlbear in Wave 1 and no additional creature in Wave 3. The three-wave battle is still dangerous because the party receives little or no downtime between waves.

For larger or higher-level parties, add the listed support creatures, increase the number of lair effects, or use the appropriate suneater owlbear statistics from the source adventure.

The characters should have access to healing, enough equipment for an extended fight, and a way to create magical darkness. Darkness prevents the owlbear from benefiting from bright sunlight and is especially valuable during the encounter.

# Harvesting

If using [[Heliana's Guide to Monster Hunting]], the owlbear provides the normal components for a fey creature, plus the following unique materials:

- A loose suneater featherfrond.
- Fey owlbear flesh.
- A pouch of radiant feathers.
- A suneater beak.
- Suneater sinew.
