# Free League of Stickyickistan Character Creation Guide

Welcome to the **Free League of Stickyickistan**: a rotating-table, West Marches / Adventurers League-style campaign where characters can join different expeditions, work with different parties, and grow through shared consequences in the same living setting.

This guide is meant to help you create a character who is ready for **any Free League adventure**, not just a specific mission.

## Quick Start

- Create a **Level 3** character.
- Use any official species, class, subclass, or background, unless the DM says otherwise.
- No evil characters. Your character must be able to cooperate with a party.
- Come prepared with a reason your character accepts dangerous contracts, expeditions, investigations, or rescue work.
- Roll ability scores before game day with the DM or use standard array / point buy.
- Choose starting equipment from your class and background, then choose the bonus items listed in this guide.
- Pick a faction, patron, employer, homeland, faith, debt, mystery, or personal goal that ties you to Stickyickistan.

## Campaign Style

The Free League is built for a **drop-in, mission-based campaign**.

Each adventure is assumed to be a job, expedition, bounty, investigation, rescue, relic hunt, diplomatic errand, monster contract, dungeon delve, or emergency call for capable adventurers. You do not need to know every other character before play begins, but you do need to be the kind of person who can work with a temporary crew.

### Player Expectations

Your character should be:

- **Adventurous:** willing to leave safety behind for danger, discovery, coin, duty, faith, glory, revenge, or curiosity.
- **Cooperative:** able to work with strangers and avoid sabotaging the party.
- **Portable:** not dependent on one specific NPC, location, or plotline to function.
- **Setting-aware:** connected to Stickyickistan in at least one small way.
- **Non-evil:** morally complicated is fine; party-hostile is not.

### West Marches / AL-Style Assumptions

Because party composition may change from session to session:

- Characters should be ready to adventure with different allies each session.
- Long-term personal plots are welcome, but they should not prevent your character from joining unrelated missions.
- If your character earns treasure, contacts, reputation, enemies, or consequences, track them on your sheet or in your notes.
- The DM may use logs, downtime, renown, rewards, or faction consequences to keep the shared campaign coherent.
- If a rule or option causes problems for the rotating-table format, the DM may adjust it between sessions.

## Character Rules

### Level

Start at **Level 3**.

This gives every character their core identity, subclass, and enough durability to survive the sort of work the Free League accepts.

### Sources

Any official D&D species, class, subclass, or background is welcome, subject to DM approval.

If a feature, spell, or option has been revised in a newer official book, ask which version the table is using. If you are unsure whether something counts as official, ask before game day.

### Ability Scores

Choose one of the following:

- **Roll stats before game day** with the DM or in an approved public channel.
- Use **standard array**.
- Use **point buy**, if approved by the DM.

If you have not rolled before the session starts, use standard array so play can begin on time.

### Alignment and Party Fit

No evil characters.

Your character can be selfish, haunted, ruthless, skeptical, criminal, or politically messy, but they must be able to:

- Accept jobs with a party.
- Share the spotlight.
- Avoid betraying, robbing, or undermining other player characters.
- Respect table safety and the DM’s campaign tone.

### Advancement

The campaign may use milestone advancement, session-based advancement, XP, or an AL-style checkpoint system. The important part is that characters of similar level can be matched for appropriate adventures.

If you miss a session, ask the DM whether catch-up advancement, downtime, or alternate rewards are available.

## Building a Stickyickistan Character

You do not need a huge backstory. A strong Free League character can be built from four answers:

1. **Why do you take dangerous work?** Coin, faith, knowledge, duty, exile, debt, thrill-seeking, justice, revenge, monster hunting, relic recovery, or survival.
2. **Who do you answer to?** A faction, temple, guild, crew, noble patron, family, commander, debt holder, mentor, spirit, or no one but yourself.
3. **What do you want from Stickyickistan?** A cure, a fortune, a title, forbidden lore, absolution, a lost heirloom, land, citizenship, revenge, or proof of a theory.
4. **Why will strangers trust you in a fight?** Reputation, oath, professionalism, shared enemy, useful skills, divine calling, contract, or mutual necessity.

## Stickyickistanian Factions and Background Hooks

You may belong to one of these groups, work for them, oppose them, owe them, spy on them, or simply have opinions about them. These are setting hooks, not required backgrounds.

### Tribunal Highblade Coalition, or THC

The **Tribunal Highblade Coalition** is the premier mercenary house of Stickyickistan. Its public image is disciplined, lawful, honorable, and protective. Many members follow the **Faith of the Sacred Flame**, though not all do.

Good fits include soldiers, paladins, clerics, fighters, rangers, warlocks bound by contract, bards serving as heralds, rogues as scouts, and almost any class with a professional military or security angle.

**Character questions:**

- Are you a true believer, a paid blade, a reformer, or a deserter trying to make amends?
- Did THC protect your home, occupy it, or fail it?
- What order would you refuse to follow?

### Seekers of the Vein

The **Seekers of the Vein** are more than a Mining and Jeweler’s Guild. They are an economic empire whose work fuels Stickyickistan’s magitech, trade, excavation, and relic industries.

They are often practical, ambitious, non-religious or religiously neutral, and willing to work with many sides if the law, profit, or discovery allows it.

Good fits include artificers, wizards, rogues, bards, fighters, rangers, clerics of craft or knowledge, and anyone tied to exploration, logistics, research, or commerce.

**Character questions:**

- Are you a guild employee, contractor, prospector, scholar, guard, accountant, surveyor, or whistleblower?
- What did you find that someone wants buried?
- Do you believe progress is worth the cost?

### Ashen Shadow Society, or ASS

The **Ashen Shadow Society** is a clandestine bounty hunting guild that “doesn’t exist.” It collects relics, bounties, secrets, monsters, and people who thought they were untouchable.

Members often pose as Seekers contractors, THC sellswords, pilgrims, caravan guards, or independent adventurers. They tend toward lawful neutral or pragmatic alignments, but player characters cannot be evil.

THC and ASS actively oppose each other. The Seekers of the Vein work with both when it is profitable, deniable, or legally convenient.

**Character questions:**

- Are you an active agent, rogue asset, informant, hunter, cleaner, or escaped recruit?
- What name do you use in public?
- What bounty, relic, or secret are you not supposed to talk about?

### Order of the Emerald Breath

The **Order of the Emerald Breath** protects sacred natural places, old rites, living spirits, and communities tied to the **Old Faiths**. The order is disciplined, lawful good, and uncompromising about certain taboos.

Good fits include monks, druids, rangers, clerics, paladins, barbarians, and any character who treats nature, breath, balance, or ancestral duty as sacred.

**Character questions:**

- Were you raised in the order, recruited after a vision, or assigned as an outside ally?
- What natural law or sacred site are you sworn to protect?
- Where do you draw the line between justice and vengeance?

### Independent Free Leaguer

You can also ignore the major factions and make your own connection.

Examples:

- A caravan guard who survived one impossible road.
- A scholar cataloging ruins older than the settler nations.
- A monster hunter with a family recipe for corpse disposal.
- A local guide who knows which hills whisper back.
- A disgraced noble looking for a second reputation.
- A medic, priest, or hedge witch following disaster zones.
- A criminal trying to go legitimate without giving up useful habits.
- A foreign adventurer who came for coin and stayed for unfinished business.

## Lore Context

### Faith of the Sacred Flame

The **Faith of the Sacred Flame** is common among the settler population of Stickyickistan. Historically, some of its institutions and followers used religion to justify conquest, displacement, and violence against native peoples.

In the present day, followers vary widely: some are reformers, some are hardliners, some are ordinary believers, and some are political opportunists. The faith is majority Human, but includes Elves, Dwarves, Dragonborn, and others.

### Old Faiths

The **Old Faiths** is the settler term for neutral or good-aligned faiths outside the Sacred Flame. These traditions are diverse and may include ancestral rites, local spirits, druidic circles, nature gods, guardian saints, oral traditions, and regional pantheons.

There is tension between the Sacred Flame and the Old Faiths, but relations have been mending in some places.

### Shadow Faiths

The **Shadow Faiths** is the settler term for evil-aligned cults, powers, and forbidden rites. Followers of the Sacred Flame and the Old Faiths disagree on many things, but both broadly oppose the Shadow Faiths.

As a player character, you may have history with Shadow Faiths as a survivor, investigator, defector, curse-bearer, hunter, or scholar, but you cannot be an evil cultist working against the party.

## Helpful Skills

Any character can be useful, and no skill is required. That said, adventures in Stickyickistan often reward characters who can investigate, survive, negotiate, identify threats, and understand strange magic.

Especially useful proficiencies include:

- **Arcana:** magitech, curses, relics, magical hazards, arcane creatures.
- **History:** ruins, factions, old wars, titles, settlements, colonization, lost records.
- **Investigation:** clues, traps, hidden mechanisms, crime scenes, research.
- **Nature:** beasts, plants, terrain, weather, natural hazards, monstrosities.
- **Perception:** ambushes, tracks, hidden doors, distant movement, suspicious details.
- **Persuasion or Deception:** contracts, faction politics, witnesses, negotiations.
- **Religion:** Sacred Flame, Old Faiths, Shadow Faiths, undead, rites, taboos.
- **Stealth:** scouting, infiltration, escapes, ambushes.
- **Survival:** navigation, tracking, foraging, wilderness travel, monster signs.

Potions count as your **1 uncommon magic item**.

If an item seems too strong in play or creates problems for the rotating-table format, the DM may replace, restrict, or revise it between adventures.

## Arcannabis

Do not use the homebrewed magic items in D&D Beyond labeled **Arcannabis**. They are deprecated and no longer fit the setting.

Instead, each character may choose **1 zip of a novice strain** from the list below. A zip is a campaign item, not a D&D Beyond magic item unless the DM says otherwise.

### Novice Strains

#### Grave Gaze #4, Undead Eyes

You know the location of any undead within **10 feet** of you that is not protected from divination magic.

#### Wight Widow, Undead Blood

The first time each turn that you hit an undead creature, the attack deals bonus damage to the target of the same type as the attack.

Ask the DM for the current novice bonus damage die before play.

#### God's Green Crack, Celestial Bones

You gain **1d6 temporary hit points** at the start of each minute.

#### Death Bubba, Undead Fat

Whenever you take necrotic damage, roll **1d4** and subtract the result from that damage.

#### Hawkeye Haze, Beast Eyes

You gain a **+1 bonus** to Perception checks.

#### Brainwreck, Aberration Brain

You can communicate telepathically with creatures within **30 feet** of you. You must be able to see the creature and share a language with it to communicate in this way.

## Adventure Readiness Checklist

Before your first session, make sure you have:

- A completed Level 3 character sheet.
- Ability scores chosen by approved method.
- Class, subclass, species, background, and equipment selected.
- Spells, prepared spells, invocations, infusions, maneuvers, or similar choices filled in.
- One reason to accept dangerous Free League work.
- One reason to cooperate with strangers.
- One connection to Stickyickistan.
- Optional common magic item chosen.
- Optional uncommon magic item chosen.
- Optional novice Arcannabis strain chosen.
- A short character pitch you can explain in one minute.

## One-Minute Character Pitch Template

Use this if you want a quick format:

> I am **[name]**, a **[species] [class/subclass]** who works as **[background/faction/role]**. I take Free League jobs because **[motivation]**. I am connected to Stickyickistan through **[faction/place/person/faith/event]**. In a party, I am useful because **[combat role/skill/personality strength]**, but trouble follows me because **[complication]**.

## Final Note

The goal is not to make the “perfect” character. The goal is to make someone fun, flexible, and ready to take a contract when the Free League bell rings.
