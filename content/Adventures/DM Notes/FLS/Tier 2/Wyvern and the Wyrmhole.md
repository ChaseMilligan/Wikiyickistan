*An Ashen Shadow Society adventure for four to five 5th-level characters*

**Wyvern and the Wyrmhole** is optimized for five 5th-level characters. The adventure should take approximately four hours to complete.

The characters are discreetly hired by the [[Ashen Shadow Society]] through an ad in [[The Roach Clip]] to hunt a [[Wyvern]] in the wilderness. The contract appears to be a straightforward monster hunt, but the Society has a second objective: use a [[Mind Blade]] and the wyvern’s preserved corpse to create an aberrant entity called a [[Bicephal Wyrmhole]]. The characters must survive the ritual, defeat the resulting creature, and recover its claws for study and weapon development.

# Introduction

## Adjusting Encounters

The adventure assumes four to five 5th-level characters. If the party has fewer characters, limited access to magical equipment, or little experience fighting airborne monsters, reduce the number of environmental hazards and allow additional opportunities to recover. If the party is especially powerful, increase the wyvern’s hit points, add environmental complications, or introduce additional scavengers during the ritual.

The adventure’s central challenge is not only combat. The characters must preserve the wyvern’s corpse, protect the ritual site, and prepare for an aberration that immediately attacks when it forms.

## Key Plot Points

**Part 1: The Contract.** The characters receive a field assignment from the Ashen Shadow Society and prepare a disposable Mind Blade for the operation.

**Part 2: The Wyvern Hunt.** The characters choose a hunting environment, track a wyvern, survive an ambush, and pursue it to its lair.

**Part 3: The Wyrmhole Rite.** The characters preserve the wyvern’s corpse, perform the ritual, and prepare for the creature’s transformation.

**Conclusion: Harvest and Return.** The characters defeat the Bicephal Wyrmhole, recover its claws, and return the harvest to the Society.

## Preparation

Before running the adventure, prepare as follows:

- Read the Adventure Background and review the Society’s objectives.
- Review the [[Wyvern]] and [[Bicephal Wyrmhole]] stat blocks.
- Prepare rules for tracking, flying creatures, falling damage, difficult terrain, and corpse preservation.
- Review the [[Mind Blade]] and the [[Graviturgic Warp-hilt]] if the party is expected to receive or craft them.
- Choose or prepare maps for the road, the wyvern’s lair, and the post-ritual battlefield.

## Content Warning

Monster hunting, body horror, surgical imagery, corpse transformation, aberrations, gravity-based hazards, falling damage, and experimental biomancy.

## Adventure Background

The [[Ashen Shadow Society]] has learned that a rare aberrant weapon attachment can be created from the claws of a [[Bicephal Wyrmhole]]. The creature cannot be found naturally. It must be created by binding a [[Mind Blade]] to the corpse of a freshly slain wyvern while the corpse still retains its magical essence.

The Society wants the resulting creature neutralized and harvested. The operation is deliberately dangerous because the experiment requires a living field team rather than a laboratory. A controlled environment would not reveal how the Wyrmhole behaves when exposed to hunters, hostile terrain, or a damaged ritual site.

The Society does not require [[Dr. Cessix Rothvell]] to participate, but he is useful as an NPC. The Mind Blade is supplied by a mind flayer or obtained from an existing source, and its intended wielder must be either the mind flayer that created it or one of that mind flayer’s thralls. Any other character can carry the blade and use it as a normal sword, but cannot attune to it or access its magic. The rite can be performed by any character who can carry out the required arcane work; the ritual does not change who the blade is attuned to. Rothvell can appear as an optional adviser, rival researcher, or replacement party member, but the adventure does not depend on him.

# Part 1: The Contract

*Estimated Duration: 30 minutes*

The characters receive the assignment through a Society sending, or an ad in [[The Roach Clip]]. The Society presents the mission as a monster hunt and reveals the ritual objective only after the characters agree to the contract.

## Adventure Hook

Before the Society summons the characters to the Ashen Citadel, the assignment may first appear in [[The Roach Clip]], Stickyickistan’s underground classifieds network.

The advertisement leads the characters to **the third rusted shrine-box along the Sootsteps in [[Upper South Booftown]]**, a soot-stained stairway shrine wedged between stacked tenements, rope bridges, and rooftop shortcuts. Locals leave burnt incense, copper bits, cheap liquor, and handwritten grudges there for whatever street spirit still listens.

To open the dead drop, a character must knock twice, whisper **“Gravity squeezes harder,”** and lift the hidden compartment beneath the offering tray. Inside is a black envelope and a long sealed case containing the Mind Blade. No Society representative appears. A second document inside the envelope describes the creature the Society wants them to create and harvest.

After the players finish reading or hearing the contract, provide the following out-of-character information:

- The Society supplies the Mind Blade for the rite.
- The contract includes a marked map to a known mountain wyvern nesting area.
- The contract includes three practical wyvern-hunting tips:
  - **Watch the sky.** Wyverns are ambush predators that prefer to strike from above and may retreat if prey proves too dangerous.
  - **Respect the stinger.** A wyvern’s tail has longer reach than its bite and delivers a painful poison that can quickly turn a fair fight ugly.
  - **Do not let it carry anyone off.** Wyverns often weaken prey, seize it, and drag or fly it back to a cavernous lair to eat later.
- The contract comes with instructions for the Rite of Mind-Corpse Convergence.
- The Mind Blade was created by a mind flayer and can be carried by the party, but only its intended mind flayer or one of that mind flayer’s thralls can attune to it or use its magical properties.
- In any other creature’s hands, it functions as a normal sword of its kind.
- The rite starts by plunging the Mind Blade into the heart of a wyvern before its magical essence fades.
- The ritual requires continuous physical contact with the corpse or blade for 10 minutes, followed by a DC 15 Arcana check while the rite is incanted from the instructions.
- On a successful check, the Mind Blade is destroyed and the corpse transforms into a [[Bicephal Wyrmhole]] over 1 minute.
- If done correctly, the Mind Blade will be consumed by the Wyrmhole.
- The Society’s primary harvest target is the Wyrmhole’s claws; additional organs, residues, or magical materials are optional bonuses.
- The Society imposes a formal time limit of 3 days to completion before they start offering the contract to other monster hunters.

#### The Shrine

The **third shrine-box** on the Sootsteps is an old neighborhood offering shrine, not an official temple. It predates most of the newer tenements around it and likely began as a small urban shrine to [The Three Sisters](app://obsidian.md/The%20Three%20Sisters), the old halfling nature goddesses of pre-colonization [Stickyickistan](app://obsidian.md/Stickyickistan). Its three rusted compartments once represented the Sisters: one for [Ashalla](app://obsidian.md/Ashalla) and prayers for family, food, and safety; one for [Darana](app://obsidian.md/Darana) and pleas for balance, fair weather, and clean dealings; and one for [Shadash](app://obsidian.md/Shadash) and the ugly prayers people only make when they have been wronged. In [Upper South Booftown](app://obsidian.md/Upper%20South%20Booftown), where ash, hunger, gang law, and bad luck all mingle together, the third box is the one people still use most.

Locals do not call it a shrine to Shadash too loudly, especially around followers of [The Faith of the Sacred Flame](app://obsidian.md/The%20Faith%20of%20the%20Sacred%20Flame), but everyone knows what the third box is for. People leave burnt incense, copper bits, cheap liquor, ash-marked flowers, poison thorns, and folded notes naming debts, betrayals, illnesses, or enemies. Some ask Shadash to take their pain away. Others ask her to pass it along to someone who deserves it more.

The map removes the need to find the correct region, but not the need to track the wyvern within it. The party still must follow signs of the creature, avoid wasting time, and determine whether to confront it at its lair or draw it into a safer battlefield.

# Part 2: The Wyvern Hunt

*Estimated Duration: 90 minutes*

The Society’s map leads the party to a known wyvern nesting area in the mountains of [[The Outback]]. This saves time by removing the “which region?” decision, but tracking still matters once the party reaches the hunting grounds. The map identifies old nests, likely feeding routes, and a few safe approaches, but the wyvern is still a living predator with a territory, habits, and a reason to move.

Use the tracking stage to answer three questions quickly:

1. **Where is the wyvern now?**
2. **Does the party learn anything useful before the fight?**
3. **Does the wyvern notice the party before they are ready?**

The Society’s map gets the party to the correct mountain territory, but the characters still need to locate the active nest, interpret signs of the wyvern’s movement, and avoid being ambushed from above. Keep this stage fast, focused, and consequential.

### Setting the Tracking Challenge

Use **three fixed Tracking checks**. Do not add extra checks for failures. Instead, each check determines how cleanly the party advances through the hunt and whether they gain useful information or suffer complications before the wyvern fight.

Divide the hunt into three quick areas:

| Check | Area | What It Represents |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | The approach trail | Reaching the marked territory and spotting signs of wyvern activity |
| 2 | The feeding grounds | Reading kills, droppings, drag marks, broken branches, or frightened wildlife |
| 3 | The nesting cliffs | Finding the active lair or choosing a safer place to draw the wyvern out |

Each Tracking check represents about **1 hour** of careful travel, searching, scouting, and interpreting signs. The full tracking stage usually takes **3 hours in-world**, plus any extra time the party spends resting, setting bait, casting rituals, or preparing an ambush.

A success gives the party useful information, a better approach, or a chance to prepare. A failure still moves the hunt forward, but causes a complication from the **Tracking Complications** table and usually adds **1 extra hour** to the hunt. A natural 1 or failure by 10 or more causes a complication, adds **1 extra hour**, and also gives the wyvern **1 Hunt Advantage**.

Surpassing the DC by 10 or more grants an additional benefit from the **Quick Success Benefits** table. This rewards excellent tracking without shortening the scene or changing the number of checks.

### Making Tracking Checks

After the party chooses an environment, ask how the characters approach the hunt. They might follow tracks, study feeding grounds, question local creatures, survey the terrain, imitate the wyvern’s call, use bait, or rely on magical tracking. Work with the players to choose an appropriate ability and skill or tool, then set the DC according to the method and circumstances.

Use these suggested DCs:

- **DC 11:** Straightforward tracking through favorable terrain, identifying a wyvern’s feeding pattern, or asking local creatures for information.
- **DC 16:** Following tracks across difficult ground, locating a hidden nest, imitating a wyvern’s call, or surveying a large area quickly.
- **DC 21:** Following a flying creature by studying its kills and droppings, tracking it across bare rock or after a storm, or using an unusual but plausible method.

Players may use different abilities with the same skill or tool when their approach supports it. To encourage varied plans, the DM may prevent the party from repeating the same ability and skill or tool combination until two other combinations have been attempted.

A Tracking check can represent any amount of time, from a few minutes to several days. The DM should shorten the interval when encounters are frequent and lengthen it during long journeys through empty terrain.

### Tracking Consequences

On a successful check, describe the changing landscape and give the party one useful benefit. On a failed check, the party still progresses, but the wyvern or the environment gains leverage. Avoid adding full extra combats unless you have time; use complications, hazards, and foreshadowing instead.

Track the party’s total successes and the wyvern’s **Hunt Advantage**.

## Tracking Complications

When the party fails a Tracking check, roll or choose one complication. These should cost time, resources, positioning, or safety without turning into a full extra combat.

| d4 | Complication |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Bad Ground.** Loose scree, a thin ledge, or a sudden rockslide forces the party into a risky crossing. Each character must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or take minor bludgeoning damage and start the wyvern encounter without ideal footing. |
| 2 | **Poisoned Kill Site.** The party finds a half-eaten carcass tainted by wyvern venom. A character inspecting it too closely must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or suffer mild poison symptoms until the next short rest. |
| 3 | **False Nest.** The party follows signs to an abandoned nest littered with bones and stolen junk. They lose time, and the wyvern gains 1 Hunt Advantage unless the party immediately changes tactics. |
| 4 | **Shadow Overhead.** The wyvern passes somewhere above the clouds or cliff line. The party catches only a glimpse, but it may have caught their scent. The next Tracking check is made with disadvantage unless the party hides, masks their scent, or waits for better cover. |

## Tracking Outcomes

| Result After 3 Checks | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 3 successes | The party reads the hunt perfectly. They find the wyvern before it spots them and may choose the battlefield, set bait, hide, or prepare for 10 minutes before initiative. |
| 2 successes | The party handles the hunt well. They locate the active nesting cliffs normally, and neither side has a major advantage unless Hunt Advantage says otherwise. |
| 1 success | The party finds the wyvern, but their approach is messy. The wyvern starts alert, suspicious, or already airborne. |
| 0 successes | The party reaches the lair the hard way. The wyvern has definitely noticed them and begins the encounter in a favorable position. |

## Hunt Advantage

The wyvern gains **1 Hunt Advantage** whenever the party critically fails a Tracking check, makes excessive noise, leaves obvious bait unattended, or spends too long resting in its territory.

Spend Hunt Advantage during the wyvern encounter as follows:

| Hunt Advantage | Effect |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The wyvern starts airborne instead of perched or grounded. |
| 2 | The wyvern has already circled the party; it begins combat with cover, concealment, or an unexpected angle of attack. |
| 3+ | The wyvern attempts to isolate a vulnerable target before initiative, such as a mount, hireling, familiar, or separated character. |

## Quick Success Benefits

When a character succeeds on a Tracking check, choose or roll one benefit.

| d4 | Benefit |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The party identifies a safe approach and avoids loose scree, thin ledges, or a dangerous climb. |
| 2 | The party finds evidence of the wyvern’s feeding pattern and knows it prefers strafing attacks before landing. |
| 3 | The party discovers old remains from past victims; they can salvage a minor useful item or clue. |
| 4 | The party spots a wind-sheltered ambush site, giving them a good place to hide, set bait, or prepare.

## The Trail Assault

The wyvern attacks the characters while they are following its trail. It targets the most exposed character first, especially a character who resembles its usual prey.

The wyvern fights like an opportunistic aerial predator. It uses its 80-foot fly speed, fangs, venomous stinger, and high terrain to strafe exposed targets, poison or weaken them, and isolate one creature from the group. It usually lands only to finish off a badly wounded target or seize prey it thinks it can carry away.

The wyvern does not fight to the death in the open unless cornered. If the characters fight back effectively, take flight, seriously wound it, or deny it an easy target, the wyvern retreats toward its lair in search of a more favorable battlefield.

When the wyvern retreats, the characters must follow it to its lair. Do not add a full extra tracking stage; instead, use the party’s existing tracking results and any Hunt Advantage to determine how favorable the lair approach is. If you want one final check, call for a single DC 16 Wisdom (Survival) or Wisdom (Perception) check. On a success, the characters reach the lair before the wyvern fully settles. On a failure, they lose 1 hour and the wyvern gains 1 Hunt Advantage.

## Wyvern Lair

The wyvern’s lair is a high cavern, cliffside hollow, or ruined watchpost with a wide opening large enough for the creature to fly through. It has chosen the site for visibility, vertical space, and easy escape routes rather than comfort. The floor is littered with bones, torn hides, splintered carts, broken tack, cracked chests, scraps of armor, and the remains of previous prey dragged here to be eaten in safety.

The lair should make the wyvern feel like an aerial ambusher, not a dragon defending a treasure hoard. It uses ledges, ceiling height, wind, and narrow approaches to stay mobile while forcing the characters to deal with bad footing and exposed positions. Most wyverns do not hoard treasure intentionally, but useful loot may be mixed into the debris from victims, livestock caravans, small boats, or wagons carried off with prey.

When the characters enter the lair, the wyvern may regain **2d10 hit points** if it had time to retreat, settle, and recover. It fights more aggressively here, but it is still an animalistic opportunist. It defends its food, territory, and escape route, but it does not need to fight to the death if an obvious way out remains. If reduced to low hit points and given a clear flight path, it may try to flee deeper into the mountains unless the characters corner it or force the final confrontation.

Possible lair hazards include:

- **High ledges.** The wyvern perches above the party and drops onto exposed targets.
- **Deep shafts and sudden drops.** A shoved, grappled, or careless creature risks falling.
- **Loose stone and unstable scree.** Fast movement may trigger slides or difficult terrain.
- **Bone piles and prey debris.** The ground is difficult terrain and may hide useful salvage.
- **Narrow approaches.** The party may have to enter single file while the wyvern has room to fly.
- **Crosswinds, smoke, steam, or volcanic heat.** Ranged attacks and flight may be complicated by the terrain.
- **Trapped food stores.** Half-dead prey, scavengers, or venom-tainted carcasses may distract or endanger the party.

## Preserving the Corpse

The characters must preserve the corpse before leaving the lair or beginning the ritual. They may use magic, alchemy, cold, embalming, or another appropriate method.

If the characters fail to preserve the corpse, the Society requires them to locate and slay another wyvern. The DM may treat this as a second hunt or allow the party to recover by securing a fresh corpse from the same region.

The corpse’s magical essence is consumed by the ritual. Effects used to preserve the corpse do not carry over to the Bicephal Wyrmhole.

# Part 3: The Wyrmhole

*Estimated Duration: 60 minutes*

The characters perform the ritual wherever they have secured the corpse. A lair is the safest location because it offers shelter and defensive terrain, but the party may choose another site.

## General Features

The ritual site should include several useful features:

- A stable surface for the corpse.
- Enough room for the party to defend the ritual.
- Cover or elevated ground.
- A hazard that can become dangerous when gravity changes.
- At least one route for retreat.

## Rite Procedure

1. The Mind Blade is plunged into the wyvern’s heart.
2. The ritual leader maintains physical contact with the corpse or blade for 10 minutes.
3. At the end of the casting, the ritual leader makes a DC 15 Arcana check.
4. On a failure, the binding fizzles. The ritual can be attempted again after another 10 minutes if the corpse still retains its magical essence.
5. On a success, the Mind Blade is destroyed and the corpse begins to transform.

## The Emergence Window

The transformation takes 1 minute. During this time, the party may reposition, cast spells, set traps, administer healing, or prepare a defensive formation.

Read or paraphrase the following:

> The sword sinks into the corpse as though the wyvern’s flesh has become deep water. The blade disappears to the hilt, followed by the handle and the hand holding it.
>
> The corpse bends in a direction that bones should not bend. Its wings fold inward, its skull splits, and the air begins to pull toward a point inside its chest.
>
> Stones rise from the ground and vanish into the forming darkness. The cave groans around you.

## Bicephal Wyrmhole

Use the [[Bicephal Wyrmhole]] stat block. This aberrant creature lashes out immediately, and attacks the nearest creature or object and continues fighting until destroyed or no longer able to act.

The party’s primary objective is to survive and defeat the creature. The Society’s secondary objective is to recover its claws, but the DM should make clear that characters may retreat if the battle becomes impossible.

## Development

If the party defeats the Wyrmhole, its body begins to destabilize. The characters have a limited period to harvest components before the remains collapse into inert residue. The claws are the highest-priority components, while the party may also recover psychic tissue, warped bone, fragments of the Mind Blade, or material from the gravity well.

If the party retreats, the Society may send another squad to finish the operation. The consequences depend on how much evidence the characters leave behind and whether the Wyrmhole escapes into the wilderness.

# Conclusion

Read or paraphrase the following when the characters return to the Society:

> The Ashen Citadel receives the harvested claws in a sealed case. Researchers gather around the table as the last of the gravity distortion fades from the samples.
>
> No one congratulates you immediately. They are too busy measuring the claws, checking the residue, and arguing over whether the Wyrmhole was a success.
>
> At last, someone closes the case.
>
> “Excellent. Now we know it can be done.”

The Society pays the agreed bounty and records the party’s performance. Characters who recover additional components receive additional compensation or future access to Society resources.

The claws are intended to create a [[Graviturgic Warp-hilt]], a socketable weapon component that manipulates gravity and pulls enemies toward its wielder.

# Rewards

The party receives the following rewards for completing the assignment:

- The agreed Society bounty.
- The recipe for crafting a [[Graviturgic Warp-hilt]].
- Additional payment for recovering useful organs, residues, or magical materials.
- Favor/Renown with the [[Ashen Shadow Society]].
- Access to future monster-utilization contracts.

## Story Award: Society Asset

The characters earn the trust of [[Ashen Shadow Society Creature Utilisation & Neutralisation Taskforce|ASSCUNT]]. When they seek specialized equipment, monster-hunting information, or access to a controlled research site, the Society may provide assistance if doing so does not compromise an active operation.

## Experience

Award experience for defeating the wyvern, surviving the ritual, defeating the Bicephal Wyrmhole, and recovering the primary harvest. If using milestone advancement, completing the adventure may advance the characters toward 6th level.

# Rumors

Before beginning the hunt, the party may gather information from hunters, Society contacts, or wilderness guides.

## Common Rumors

- Wyverns are more intelligent than most city hunters believe.
- A wyvern that flees from a fight is probably leading its attackers toward its mate or lair.
- The Ashen Shadow Society pays more for claws than for an intact hide.
- Some mountains in the Outback contain caves where sound and gravity behave strangely.

## Reliable Rumors

- The Society is testing a new weapon component made from aberrant claws.
- The ritual requires a corpse with its magical essence intact.
- The resulting creature has no loyalty to the people who created it.
- A prepared battlefield is more valuable than a powerful weapon during the final encounter.

## Dangerous Rumors

- The Wyrmhole can be controlled if the ritual leader keeps hold of the Mind Blade.
- The Society will abandon the party if the experiment fails.
- The Bicephal Wyrmhole is only the first stage of a larger transformation.
- The claws can be harvested safely without defeating the creature.
