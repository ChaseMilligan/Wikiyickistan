#Alive #ASS 

[Stat Block](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/273036-turbulence)

**Race**: [[Tiefling]]

**Rank**: 1

**Renown**: 3

**Personality**: Charismatic and cunning, Velindra has a flair for the dramatic. She’s prone to exaggeration and loves spinning tales of her (sometimes questionable) exploits.

**Encounter**: Velindra can be found at [[The Rusted Chalice]] in the evenings. She enjoys playing blackjack, and will be friendly if PC's decide to play with her. She also might happen to approach them, whatever feels right. Velindra has some info about an [[Abandoned Chapel Ruins]] in [[The Outback]]. She will hype this place up a lot saying that it seemed like a free raid with just a few [[Goblins]] hanging around, and a load of loot. She describes some pathetic looking creatures guarding a hoard of gold coins and other items that one would expect from a Dragon. She says that if they give her 50gp she will be willing to spill the beans on where it is. If questioned, she will say she was alone when she came upon it, so she left cause it didn't look that easy, but with a friend or two it seemed like a piece of cake. Giant piles of gold coins and gems, she will say. A DC 17 Insight check will reveal this to mostly be a lie. She does indeed know of an abandoned chapel in the Outback guarded by small creatures, but the amount of wealth they have has been wildly exaggerated, and the creatures guarding it may or may not be Goblins.
#### **Scene Setup**

*As the neon glow of **The Rusted Chalice** hums against the medieval stone walls, the atmosphere is thick with smoke, laughter, and the low hum of conversation. In the corner of the tavern, nestled near the blackjack table, sits **Velindra**—a striking [[Tiefling]] with crimson skin and golden eyes that gleam under the pulsing neon lights. Dressed in dark, stylish leathers adorned with small trinkets and charms, she shuffles a deck of cards idly between rounds, a relaxed smirk ever-present on her lips.*

If the **party joins her at the table**, she greets them with a playful smirk.

> **"Well now, fresh faces at my table? That means fresh luck. Pull up a seat—maybe it'll be your night to walk away rich. Or maybe it'll be mine."**

She plays confidently, a mix of skill and luck carrying her through each round. As the game progresses, she warms up, engaging in casual conversation. She watches the party closely, gauging them as much as she enjoys the game. After a while—whether through conversation or on her own initiative—she brings up **a certain abandoned chapel**.

---

### **The Hook: The Abandoned Chapel in the Outback**

*Velindra leans back in her chair, swirling a drink in her hand as she lowers her voice just slightly, enough to be heard over the tavern’s noise but keeping things **just between them**.*

> **"Y'know, you lot look like the type who might be interested in a little... side venture. I happened across something real special the other day. A ruin, way out in the Outback. Old chapel, abandoned as far as I could tell. But get this—"** she grins, setting her glass down with a soft clink.  
> **"The place had a hoard. I mean, I'm talkin' piles of gold, gems, weapons, trinkets—looked like something a dragon would sit on, 'cept there was no dragon. Just a few scrawny little goblins poking around, barely looking like they knew what to do with it."**

She makes a dismissive wave, laughing lightly.

> **"I was alone, right? And sure, I'm quick, but even I ain't reckless enough to take on a place like that without backup. Seemed like too much hassle for one person. But a crew? Couple of sharp blades? That could be an easy payday. Like, ‘free loot’ kind of easy."**

She lets that sink in, watching their expressions.

> **"Now, obviously, a tip like that ain't free. But hey, you seem like you could make good use of it. Tell you what—**50 gold, and I’ll give you the exact location.** Call it a finder's fee. It's a **steal** for the kind of haul waiting for you out there."**

---

### **If Pressed for Details or Questioned**

If the party starts **pressing her with questions**, trying to poke holes in her story, she holds her ground.

- **What kind of creatures were guarding it?**
    
    > **"Like I said—some pathetic little things. Goblins, probably. You know how they scurry about, fussin’ over shiny things they don’t deserve."**
    
- **If it was so easy, why didn’t she take anything?**
    
    > **"Alone? Come on, I’m good, but I’m not stupid. You ever tried hauling sacks of treasure all by yourself while keeping an eye on the room? A partner or two makes that _way_ easier."**
    
- **What proof does she have?**
    
    > **She laughs. "What, you expect me to lug a chest of gold back here just to prove a point? Look, I'm giving you a golden opportunity—literally. If you don't want it, hey, someone else will."**
    

If the **party rolls a DC 17 Insight check**, they realize she’s **stretching the truth**. There **is** a ruin, and it **is** guarded by small creatures—but the whole “hoard of dragon-like treasure” is **definitely exaggerated**. The creatures might not even be goblins.

If called out, Velindra shrugs.

> **"Alright, so maybe 'hoard' was a _bit_ of an oversell. But gold’s gold, isn’t it? You still want the location, or not?"**

If they refuse to pay, she won’t push, but if they show interest later, she’ll still be willing to share—for a price.

---

### **Outcome & Further Interaction**

- If they **pay her**, she provides the **location of the ruins** in **The Outback**, still playing it up as a great opportunity.
- If they **call her bluff** and refuse, she just shrugs, moving on to her next game—though she might subtly warn **other bounty hunters** in the tavern that the party is suspicious.
- If they **befriend her**, she might lower the price **or** offer to come along—for an equal cut of the treasure.

---

Velindra’s a **hustler, a gambler, and a charmer**, but she’s not outright malicious. She’s playing an **angle**, but if the party’s smart, they can turn the situation to their advantage. Whether they believe her or not… well, that’s up to them.
