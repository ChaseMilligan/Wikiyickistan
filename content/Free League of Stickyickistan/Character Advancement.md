# Character Advancement

Character advancement in the [[Free League of Stickyickistan]] is designed for an open-table campaign with rotating players, rotating DMs, and shared continuity. Characters grow by completing adventures, recording rewards, and carrying their reputation forward between sessions.

## Advancement Method

The Free League uses **XP per adventure**.

Each adventure has a listed XP reward. When a character completes that adventure, they earn the listed XP and record it with their session rewards. XP is awarded for completing the adventure, not for individually counting every defeated monster, trap, or encounter.

The listed XP reward may account for:

- Expected combat encounters
- Investigation and exploration
- Major objectives
- Optional objectives
- Monster-hunting difficulty
- Adventure length
- Risk, complications, and consequences

Characters level up when their total XP reaches the normal D&D level thresholds.

## Adventure XP

Adventure briefs and DM notes should list the expected XP reward for each character.

Example:

> **Rewards:** 225 gp each, 600 XP each

For [[Heliana's Guide to Monster Hunting]]-style monster hunts, use the adventure’s listed XP award or convert the hunt into a single adventure XP reward. This keeps Heliana’s monster-hunt reward structure while avoiding the need to track every individual monster or hazard separately.

## Partial Completion

If the party does not complete the adventure, the DM may award partial XP based on meaningful progress.

| Progress | XP Award |
|---|---:|
| Minor progress | 25% of the adventure XP |
| Major progress, but objective incomplete | 50% of the adventure XP |
| Main objective complete, but major complications unresolved | 75% of the adventure XP |
| Adventure complete | 100% of the adventure XP |

Partial XP is optional. If the party abandons the job, fails the main objective, or causes a disaster, the DM may reduce or withhold XP as appropriate.

## Bonus XP

Some adventures may offer bonus XP for optional objectives, exceptional problem solving, protecting civilians, rescuing NPCs, preserving important sites, capturing instead of killing, harvesting a dangerous monster cleanly, or preventing collateral damage.

Bonus XP should be listed in the adventure rewards when possible.

Examples:

- +50 XP for rescuing a trapped NPC
- +100 XP for preserving a sacred site
- +150 XP for completing a clean monster harvest
- +250 XP for resolving a threat without starting a faction conflict

## Session Length

Session length helps determine how much XP an adventure should be worth, but characters do not automatically level after a four-hour session.

A four-hour adventure usually counts as a **standard adventure** for XP purposes. Shorter sessions may award less XP, while longer or more dangerous adventures may award more.

For multi-session adventures, the DM should tell players how XP will be awarded. Usually, XP is awarded at the end of the adventure or after a major milestone.

## Suggested XP by Adventure Tier

Use these values as a starting point when assigning XP to Free League adventures.

| Adventure Type | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 | Tier 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Short adventure / 2–3 hours | 300 XP | 1,500 XP | 5,000 XP | 12,000 XP |
| Standard adventure / 3–4 hours | 600 XP | 3,000 XP | 10,000 XP | 25,000 XP |
| Long adventure / 4–6 hours | 900 XP | 5,000 XP | 15,000 XP | 40,000 XP |
| Deadly or major adventure | 1,200 XP | 7,500 XP | 25,000 XP | 60,000 XP |

DMs may adjust these values based on difficulty, objectives, party level, and how much progress the adventure represents.

## Tier of Play

Characters are grouped into broad tiers of play.

| Tier | Character Levels | XP Range | Maximum Magic Item Rarity |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Tier 1 | 1–4 | 0–6,499 XP | Uncommon |
| Tier 2 | 5–10 | 6,500–63,999 XP | Rare |
| Tier 3 | 11–16 | 64,000–354,999 XP | Very Rare |
| Tier 4 | 17–20 | 355,000+ XP | Legendary |

A character should generally play in adventures appropriate to their tier. DMs may set tighter level ranges for specific adventures.

## Recording Advancement

At the end of each session, players should record:

- Adventure name
- Date played
- DM
- Character level before and after the session
- Total XP before and after the session
- XP earned
- Gold earned
- Magic items gained
- Consumables gained or spent
- Downtime, favors, faction changes, or story awards
- Any lasting conditions, curses, diseases, debts, or consequences

Rewards should be recorded before leaving the table whenever possible.

## Gold and Treasure

Gold, treasure, and mundane equipment are awarded as directed by the adventure.

If treasure is found during play, the party may use it during the session. At the end of the session, remaining treasure with monetary value is converted to gold and divided as the party desires.

For guidance, total treasure by adventure tier should usually fall within these ranges:

| Adventure Tier | Typical Total Treasure |
|---|---:|
| Tier 1 | 100–500 gp |
| Tier 2 | 1,000–5,000 gp |
| Tier 3 | 10,000–50,000 gp |
| Tier 4 | 50,000–100,000 gp |

## Magic Items

Characters may keep magic items found during an adventure if the item is specifically awarded by the adventure or approved by the DM. Finding a magic item in and adventure doesn't necessarily mean you get to keep it afterwards.

Items that are loaned to the party, required for an objective, destroyed by the story, or marked as temporary rewards cannot be kept unless the adventure says otherwise.

Magic items should be appropriate to the character’s tier. If an adventure randomly awards an item above the tier’s maximum rarity, reroll it or replace it with an appropriate item.

## Consumables

Potions, scrolls, ammunition, Arcannabis products, monster components, and other consumables should be recorded when gained or spent.

Consumables may be used during the adventure in which they are found unless the DM says otherwise. Any remaining consumables at the end of the session belong to the characters who receive them.

## Story Awards

Story awards represent favors, titles, access, faction standing, special permissions, curses, debts, or ongoing consequences.

Examples include:

- Trusted guest status at a location
- Access to a restricted shop, faction, or region
- A favor owed by an NPC
- Reputation with a guild or noble house
- A curse, mark, debt, or enemy
- A License or Recipe to craft a specific item from harvested monster parts that may be illegal or exceptionally rare.

Story awards should be written clearly so future DMs know what they mean.

## Downtime

Characters may use downtime between or during sessions when the DM agrees there is enough time for the activity.

Downtime can represent training, crafting, research, carousing, recovery, spell copying, faction work, business, or other between-adventure activity.

The DM has final say on whether downtime is available during a specific adventure.

## Death, Disease, and Curses

Bad things happen to adventurers.

Diseases, curses, poisons, and other lasting effects persist until removed by magic, downtime, story resolution, or the adventure’s conclusion. Characters may seek spellcasting services in settlements or through appropriate NPCs when available.

If a character dies, follow the table rules for resurrection, replacement characters, or post-adventure recovery.

## Player Responsibility

Players are responsible for keeping their own character records accurate.

DMs should clearly state rewards at the end of a session, but players should track what their character earned, spent, lost, and suffered.

If a record is unclear, the current DM may make a fair ruling based on the available notes.
