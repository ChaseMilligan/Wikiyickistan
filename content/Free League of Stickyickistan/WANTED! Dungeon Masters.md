The Free League of Stickyickistan thrives when more people step behind the screen. If you have a weird idea, a dangerous dungeon, a political disaster, a haunted tavern, or a villain with a grudge, we want you to run it.

You do **not** need to be a perfect DM. You do **not** need to write a novel. You only need enough of an adventure to give the table a fun session.

# Why Run Games?

Running adventures helps the whole League:

- More sessions can happen.
- More characters get chances to level.
- More corners of Stickyickistan get explored.
- More players get to tell stories that matter to the setting.
- DMs get to add their own weirdness to the world.

To encourage more people to run games, DMs earn rewards when they prepare and run adventures for the Free League of Stickyickistan.

# Adventure Tiers

DM rewards are based on the tier of the adventure they run.

| Adventure Tier | Character Levels | Typical Scope |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ------------- |
| Tier 1 | 1 - 4 | Local trouble, small dungeons, strange jobs, dangerous errands |
| Tier 2 | 5 - 10 | Regional threats, major monsters, faction conflict, dangerous expeditions |
| Tier 3 | 11 - 16 | World-shaking problems, planar threats, legendary villains |
| Tier 4 | 17 - 20 | Mythic conflicts, reality-threatening events, gods, archfiends, ancient powers |

# DM Reward Principles

DM rewards should be meaningful, but not so strong that they become mandatory or break the campaign.

The goals are:

- Reward the time and creativity spent preparing adventures.
- Encourage DMs to run games across all four tiers.
- Let DMs benefit their own characters without overshadowing regular play.
- Keep rewards simple enough to track.
- Make sure rewards feel connected to the League.
## DM Credits

When you run a Free League adventure, you earn **DM Credits** based on the tier of the adventure.

| Adventure Tier | DM Credits Earned |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| Tier 1 | 1 DM Credit |
| Tier 2 | 2 DM Credits |
| Tier 3 | 3 DM Credits |
| Tier 4 | 4 DM Credits |

An adventure must be meaningfully prepared and run for a table to earn DM Credits. The DM should provide enough structure for a complete session or adventure, including a clear hook, meaningful encounters, and rewards appropriate to the tier.

### Spending DM Credits

DM Credits can be spent on rewards for one of your Free League characters.

| Cost         | Reward                                                                                    |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 DM Credit  | Gain 100 gp × your character’s tier.                                                      |
| 1 DM Credit  | Gain 10 downtime days.                                                                    |
| 1 DM Credit  | Gain one common magic item.                                                               |
| 2 DM Credits | Gain one consumable magic item appropriate to your character’s tier.                      |
| 2 DM Credits | Gain one story contact, minor title, favor, or faction connection.                        |
| 3 DM Credits | Gain one uncommon magic item.                                                             |
| 4 DM Credits | Gain one rare magic item. The character must be Tier 2 or higher.                         |
| 6 DM Credits | Gain one very rare magic item. The character must be Tier 3 or higher.                    |
| 8 DM Credits | Gain one legendary magic item. The character must be Tier 4.                              |
| 4 DM Credits | Gain one level, following the normal level limits and tier restrictions of the adventure. |

DM Credit rewards are subject to DM approval. If a reward would not make sense for the character, the campaign, or the current tier of play, work with the DM to choose something similar.

### Magic Item Limits Still Apply

Magic items gained with DM Credits count against the normal number of magic items a character can bring to a session.

If a character earns more magic items than they can bring, they can keep the extras, but they must choose which ones are active for each session.

Legendary magic items may only be used in Tier 4 play.

### Leveling with DM Credits

A character may spend DM Credits to gain a level, but they cannot gain more than one level this way between sessions.

You cannot use DM Credits to level a character beyond the tier of the adventures currently being played unless the DM approves it.

DM Credit leveling should help DMs keep up with the rest of the group, not skip past.

### Material Creation Rewards

If you write an adventure for another DM to run, but you do not run it yourself, you may earn partial credit.

| Contribution                                                             | Reward                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adventure seed, hook, or encounter idea                                  | No reward, but thank you                                  |
| Usable one-page adventure outline to build off                           | 1 DM Credit                                               |
| Full adventure document another DM can run                               | DM Credits equal to half the adventure’s tier, rounded up |
| Major setting document, faction, dungeon, or region used by multiple DMs | 1 - 3 DM Credits, based on usefulness                     |

The same adventure cannot grant full DM Credits for both writing and running unless the group agrees it involved substantial separate work.

### First-Time DM Bonus

The first time someone runs a Free League adventure, they earn **+1 bonus DM Credit**.

# DM Logs

DMs should track the adventures they run and the DM Credits they earn.

A DM log should include:

- Adventure title.
- Date run.
- Tier.
- Players.
- Characters involved.
- DM Credits earned.
- Any setting additions or story consequences.
- Any DM Credits spent.

# Final Say

The Free League should be generous with DM rewards, but the group should protect the fun and balance of the campaign.

If a reward seems too strong, too vague, or too disruptive, adjust it.

The point is simple:

**Run cool games. Get cool rewards. Make Stickyickistan weirder.**

# The Cabal of Tribulation

The Dungeon Masters of the Free League are not merely referees, organizers, or adventure-writers.

They are members of **the Cabal of Tribulation**: a secretive fellowship of otherworldly beings, fate-weavers, planar bureaucrats, doom-artisans, trickster-gods, retired villains, prophetic goblins, and cosmic freaks who gather beyond the veil to design trials for the inhabitants of the planes.

In character, the Cabal is the hidden hand behind strange jobs, impossible coincidences, cursed treasure maps, conveniently unlocked dungeon doors, suspiciously helpful tavern rumors, and villains who monologue just long enough for heroes to interrupt them.

Out of character, the Cabal is the group of DMs who help create, organize, and run adventures for the Free League of Stickyickistan.

When you run a game, you are not just “DMing.”

You are convening the Cabal.

### What the Cabal Does

Members of the Cabal create tribulations for adventurers to overcome. These tribulations may include:

- Dungeons.
- Mysteries.
- Monster hunts.
- Political disasters.
- Planar accidents.
- Curses.
- Rival adventuring parties.
- Villains with extremely normal and reasonable hobbies.
- Taverns that should not exist.
- Artifacts no one should have touched.
- Moral dilemmas that were definitely avoidable.
- Goblins with access to infrastructure.

The Cabal does not exist to punish players. It exists to create glorious problems.

A good tribulation should be dangerous, interesting, and survivable enough that the heroes can make terrible decisions inside it.

### Cabal Roleplay

DMs are encouraged to treat the Cabal as an in-universe guild, secret order, or cosmic workplace.

You may give yourself a Cabal title, persona, domain, mask, office, or absurd bureaucratic role.

Examples include:

- The Minister of Unopened Doors.
- The Archivist of Bad Ideas.
- The Goblin Laureate of Consequences.
- The Baron of Scheduling Conflicts.
- The Saint of Unbalanced Encounters.
- The Cartographer of Places That Bite.

Cabal roleplay should be fun, optional, and never required to run games. It is a way for DMs to build a shared identity and make adventure creation feel like part of the setting.

### Cabal Proceedings

When a DM proposes an adventure, they may present it as a **Tribulation** before the Cabal.

A Tribulation might include:

- A title.
- The intended tier.
- The planar region, settlement, dungeon, or problem involved.
- The kind of trouble being inflicted on the heroes.
- Any factions, monsters, or NPCs involved.
- Whether the adventure adds anything permanent to the setting.

The Cabal may then offer suggestions, complications, connections to existing lore, or rewards.

This should be collaborative, not restrictive. The Cabal should help DMs make adventures stronger, not bury them in approval processes.

### Cabal Ranks

As DMs run adventures, they may gain standing within the Cabal.

| Cabal Standing | Requirement |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| Initiate of Tribulation | Run your first Free League adventure |
| Weaver of Woes | Run three Free League adventures |
| Architect of Adversity | Run at least one Tier 2 or higher adventure |
| Harbinger of Calamity | Run at least one Tier 3 or higher adventure |
| Demiurge of Disaster | Run at least one Tier 4 adventure |
| Grand Menace of the League | Run at least one adventure in each of the four tiers |

Cabal standing is mostly for fun, bragging rights, and roleplay. It may also be used as inspiration for story awards, titles, or special rewards.
