# Guildhall Crafting, Stock & Progression

The Adventurers' Guildhall grows alongside its members.

Rather than shops automatically gaining better equipment as adventurers gain levels, the Guild improves through the discoveries, treasure, monster components, recipes, and materials its members bring home.

When adventurers return from expeditions, they aren't merely earning gold. They are expanding the Guild's collective knowledge and resources.

A monster slain today may provide the recipe, component, or material that another adventuring party uses months later.

---

# The Guild Economy

Guild shops have three broad categories of inventory.

|Stock|Description|
|---|---|
|**Standard Stock**|Ordinary goods and services that the shop can reliably obtain.|
|**Guild Stock**|Physical items, components, essence, ingredients, and other limited resources currently owned by the Guild.|
|**Unlocked Recipes**|Items the Guild has learned how to manufacture, enchant, forge, brew, or otherwise create.|

The most important distinction is:

> **Recipes are permanent. Materials are not.**

Once the Guild learns how to make something, that knowledge remains available permanently.

Actually making the item still requires the necessary materials.

---

# Guild Recipes

The Guild maintains a shared **Recipe Catalogue** containing everything its craftspeople currently know how to make.

A recipe may be unlocked by recovering a written recipe or schematic, learning the technique from another craftsperson, completing certain quests, researching an item, dismantling an existing magic item, or otherwise discovering the knowledge during an adventure.

Once a recipe is unlocked, the appropriate Guild shop can begin accepting commissions for that item.

Unlocking a recipe **does not add unlimited copies of the item to the shop**.

It simply means:

> **The Guild knows how to make it.**

The adventurers must still provide, purchase, or otherwise acquire the materials necessary to create it.

---

# Reverse Engineering Magic Items

Recovered magic items can sometimes be given to an appropriate Guild craftsperson for study.

Most magical equipment is handled by **Hex**, the Guild Artificer.

A character may choose to have an eligible magic item **dismantled for research** instead of keeping or selling it.

The original item is destroyed during this process, and its recipe is permanently added to the Guild Catalogue.

This creates three meaningful choices whenever an interesting item is recovered:

| Choice           | Result                                                                              |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Keep It**      | The adventurer keeps the unique item.                                               |
| **Sell It**      | The adventurer receives payment and the physical item enters Guild Stock.           |
| **Dismantle It** | The item is sacrificed so its recipe can become permanently available to the Guild. |

The GM determines whether a particular item can reasonably be reverse engineered.

Artifacts, unique relics, divine creations, cursed objects, and similar exceptional items may not be reproducible.

---

# Selling Items to Guild Shops

Physical items sold to a Guild shop become part of that shop's **Guild Stock**.

This stock is persistent until it is purchased, consumed, destroyed, transferred, or used in crafting.

For example, if an adventurer sells Zaza three harvested troll hearts, the Apothecary now has:

> **Troll Heart ×3**

Those hearts can later be purchased or used by another Guild member.

The same applies to other magical components, essence, potions, equipment, unusual materials, monster parts, and similar useful resources.

This means selling something to the Guild can directly benefit future adventuring parties.

---

# Crafting Methods

Guild crafting uses three principal methods from the crafting rules: **Manufacturing, Enchanting, and Forging**.

## Manufacturing

Manufacturing creates a **mundane item from raw materials**.

It requires appropriate materials, tools, time, and a Manufacturing check.

As a general rule, the raw materials required to manufacture mundane equipment cost approximately **one-third of the item's normal purchase price**.

Examples include forging a sword, sewing a cloak, creating armour, building a wand's physical body, preparing a potion base, or constructing mechanical equipment.

Unusual materials can be more difficult to work with. Monster hides, dragon scales, bone, silver, mithral, adamantine, and similarly difficult substances may increase the Manufacturing DC.

The Guild Blacksmith is the primary Guild specialist for manufacturing weapons and armour.

---

## Enchanting

Enchanting turns a mundane object into a magical item.

Enchanting requires:

**A mundane base item + a magical component + essence**

The item's recipe determines which mundane item and magical component are required. The rarity of the desired item determines which essence is required.

Enchanting draws magical properties from harvested monster components and transfers or grafts that power into an object.

Only a creature with a Spellcasting ability can normally perform an Enchanting check. The skill used for the check depends upon the creature type from which the magical component originated.

**Vex** is the Guild's primary professional enchanter.

---

## Forging

Forging combines Manufacturing and Enchanting into a single creation process.

Instead of manufacturing an ordinary object and enchanting it afterward, the magical component is worked directly into the equipment as it takes shape.

Unlike ordinary Enchanting, Forging allows a non-spellcasting craftsperson to perform the magical work using the ability associated with their tool.

Forging generally requires both a Manufacturing check and an Enchanting check.

The Guild Blacksmith can eventually gain access to increasingly sophisticated Forging commissions as the Guild obtains the appropriate recipes, equipment, materials, and expertise.

---

# Essence

**Essence determines the potential power of magical crafting.**

Monster components provide an item's particular magical qualities, while essence provides the raw magical power necessary to reach a particular rarity.

|Item Rarity|Required Essence|Enchanting DC|
|---|---|--:|
|**Common**|None|12|
|**Uncommon**|Frail Essence|15|
|**Rare**|Robust Essence|18|
|**Very Rare**|Potent Essence|21|
|**Legendary**|Mythic Essence|25|
|**Artifact**|Deific Essence|30|

Essence is a **physical Guild resource**, not a permanent unlock.

If Hex has only one Robust Essence available and somebody uses it to craft a Rare item, the Guild now has zero Robust Essence until more is acquired.

This naturally limits the availability of powerful equipment without requiring arbitrary character-level restrictions.

A shop may know how to make an extremely powerful item long before the Guild possesses the essence necessary to actually create one.

---

# Crafting Time

Powerful equipment also requires substantially more time to create.

|Rarity|Consumable|Non-Attunement|Attunement|
|---|--:|--:|--:|
|**Common**|30 min.|1 hr.|2 hrs.|
|**Uncommon**|4 hrs.|10 hrs.|20 hrs.|
|**Rare**|20 hrs.|40 hrs.|80 hrs.|
|**Very Rare**|80 hrs.|160 hrs.|320 hrs.|
|**Legendary**|320 hrs.|640 hrs.|1,280 hrs.|

These times come directly from the underlying enchanting rules.

Crafting time does not need to be continuous.

As a result, powerful Guild commissions may take days, weeks, or months of downtime to complete.

---

# Monster Components

Harvested monster components are one of the most important resources in the Guild economy.

Components may serve as ingredients for magic items, potions, forged equipment, magical meals, poisons, medicines, Arcannabis, or other specialized creations.

When a previously unknown component is brought to the Guild, the appropriate specialist can study and catalogue it.

Once catalogued, the Guild understands what the component is and may reveal known recipes that use it.

A newly discovered component might therefore produce:

> ### New Guild Discovery
> 
> **Red Dragon Breath Sac**
> 
> Component identified and catalogued.
> 
> Relevant recipes are now visible in the Guild Catalogue.
> 
> Existing recipes requiring a Red Dragon Breath Sac may now be commissioned if all other materials are available.

Discovering a component does **not** give the Guild an unlimited supply of it.

Every harvested component remains an individual physical resource.

---

# Monster Specialists

Different Guild specialists have expertise with different types of creatures.

## Zaza — Apothecary

Zaza primarily handles biological, natural, and alchemical monster materials associated with:

**Beasts, Fey, Giants, Humanoids, Monstrosities, Oozes, and Plants.**

Zaza can identify and preserve components, purchase harvested materials, explain potential uses, help research recipes, prepare potion bases, produce potions and medicines, and maintain the Guild's natural-component inventory.

---

## Hex — Artificer

Hex primarily handles magical, planar, supernatural, and constructed creature materials associated with:

**Aberrations, Celestials, Constructs, Dragons, Elementals, Fiends, and Undead.**

Hex can identify magical components, research magical properties, enchant equipment, create wondrous items, reverse engineer magic items, upgrade existing magical items, and maintain the Guild's arcane-component inventory.

Guild members may purchase available components for their own crafting or use them as part of a Guild commission.

Once consumed, that stock is removed.

# Commissioning Items

If the Guild knows a recipe, a member may request that the appropriate Guild craftsperson create the item.

A commission requires all ingredients listed by the recipe.

Those ingredients may come from the player's personal inventory, Guild Stock, or a combination of both.

If a required component is unavailable, the commission cannot begin.

This can turn Guild crafting directly into an adventure hook.

> **Commission: Flame Tongue**
> 
> Recipe: Known  
> Smithing: Available  
> Potent Essence: Available  
> Red/Gold/Brass Dragon Breath Sac: **MISSING**

The missing ingredient now gives the party a reason to hunt, trade, investigate, or accept a Guild contract.

---

# Crafted Item Quality

Crafting checks can produce more than simple success or failure.

Particularly good or poor crafting results may cause the finished equipment to gain **boons or flaws**, collectively called quirks.

Manufacturing quirks affect the physical construction of an item, while Enchanting quirks affect its magical qualities. The number of magical boons an item can sustain is limited by the essence used to create it.

|Essence|Rarity|Maximum Enchanting Boons|
|---|---|--:|
|None|Common|0|
|Frail|Uncommon|1|
|Robust|Rare|2|
|Potent|Very Rare|3|
|Mythic|Legendary|3|
|Deific|Artifact|3|

Commissioning an item therefore does not necessarily guarantee a perfectly ordinary version of that item.

Exceptionally skilled work might produce something better than expected.

Poor work may leave an otherwise functional item with unusual flaws.

---

# Improving Existing Magic Items

Some magical items can be upgraded to a higher-rarity version.

To upgrade an item, the existing magic item itself can serve as both the mundane item and magical component. An essence corresponding to the desired new rarity is also required.

The appropriate Enchanting or Forging checks are then repeated at the higher rarity.

On success, the item's rarity increases.

On failure, the essence is consumed but the original magic item remains at its previous rarity.

This makes powerful essence useful even when a character already owns a favored piece of equipment.

---

# The Blacksmith

The Guild Blacksmith specializes in physical equipment.

The Blacksmith manufactures weapons, armour, shields, ammunition, and other metal goods; works with exotic mundane materials; repairs and refits equipment; incorporates harvested monster materials into mundane equipment; and eventually accepts magical Forging commissions.

A typical magical commission involving separate Manufacturing and Enchanting therefore follows:

> **Monster → Harvest Component → Identify Component → Manufacture Base Item → Enchant Item → Finished Equipment**

The Blacksmith handles the physical construction.

Vex handles the magical enchantment.

Forging allows those stages to be combined when appropriate.

---

# Zaza's Apothecary

Zaza's shop specializes in consumables, biological monster components, harvesting equipment, medicines, potions, poisons, herbs, expedition supplies, and Arcannabis cultivation.

Potion and Arcannabis recipes can be permanently unlocked like other recipes.

Individual potion ingredients, monster components, Arcannabis seeds, and harvested plant material remain consumable stock.

Zaza may also purchase unusual herbs, organs, glands, venoms, spores, blood, hides, Arcannabis strains, and other useful monster materials brought back by adventurers.

---

# Hex's Artificer Shop

Hex specializes in magical equipment and enchantment.

Hex's permanent commission catalogue expands as the Guild discovers magical recipes.

Hex can also maintain a limited amount of completed magical equipment as physical shop stock.

Therefore, seeing:

> **Pearl of Power — Recipe Known**

does not necessarily mean a Pearl of Power is currently sitting in the display case.

It means Hex knows how to make one.

By comparison:

> **Pearl of Power ×1 — In Stock**

means a finished Pearl of Power is currently available for immediate purchase.

---

# The Guild Kitchen

Monster hunting can even improve the Guild kitchen.

The crafting rules allow **Cook's Utensils** to create magical meals.

The Guild therefore maintains a shared **Cookbook** alongside its crafting catalogue.

Magical meal recipes can be discovered during adventures and permanently unlocked.

The kitchen must still possess the appropriate ingredients to prepare them.

As with other consumables, ingredients are expended when the meal is prepared.

---

# The Guild Library

The Library acts as the Guild's central repository of knowledge.

Recipes, monster research, expedition reports, maps, harvesting notes, crafting discoveries, and other useful information can be deposited here.

Players may use the Library to research monsters before an expedition, determine where useful components may be found, investigate recipes, learn about unusual materials, or search previous Guild reports.

The Library therefore represents the **knowledge side** of Guild progression while the shops represent its physical resources.

---

# Guild Discoveries

A single successful adventure can improve multiple parts of the Guildhall.

Defeating a strange creature might result in:

> ### Expedition Discoveries
> 
> **Library:** New bestiary entry recorded.
> 
> **Zaza:** New biological component identified.
> 
> **Hex:** Magical properties of another component catalogued.
> 
> **Blacksmith:** New monster material available for manufacturing.
> 
> **Kitchen:** New magical meal ingredient discovered.
> 
> **Guild Stock:** Remaining harvested components added to storage.
> 
> **Recipe Catalogue:** One or more new recipes revealed or unlocked.

The Guild therefore becomes a record of the adventures its members have completed.

---

# Shared Guild Progression

Anything permanently learned by the Guild becomes available to **all Guild members**.

If one adventuring party discovers a recipe, later adventurers can commission that item.

If one party sells useful monster components, another party can purchase them.

If somebody discovers a new magical meal, everyone can eventually order it from the kitchen.

The Guildhall is therefore a shared progression system.

Its shops begin relatively ordinary and gradually become extraordinary because of what its adventurers accomplish.

---

# Quick Reference: Bringing Loot Home

When returning to the Guildhall with unusual loot, determine what category it belongs to.

|Loot|What Can Happen|
|---|---|
|**Mundane Item**|Keep it, sell it, or use it as a crafting base.|
|**Magic Item**|Keep it, sell it into Guild Stock, or possibly dismantle it to learn its recipe.|
|**Monster Component**|Identify it, sell it, store it, or use it for crafting.|
|**Essence**|Keep it, sell it, or use it to determine the rarity of a crafted item.|
|**Recipe/Schematic**|Permanently add it to the Guild Catalogue.|
|**Rare Material**|Sell it, store it, or use it for Manufacturing/Forging.|
|**Magical Food Ingredient**|Sell/store it or use it with an unlocked Guild recipe.|
|**Book/Research/Bestiary**|Add its useful information to the Guild Library.|

---

# The Core Rule

The entire system can be summarized with one principle:

> **Adventures create resources. Resources create equipment. Discoveries create permanent Guild upgrades.**

Gold lets adventurers buy what the Guild already possesses.

Monster components and essence let them create what the Guild knows how to make.

Recipes expand what the Guild is capable of making forever.

And every expedition has the potential to leave the Guildhall more useful, knowledgeable, and powerful than it was before.
